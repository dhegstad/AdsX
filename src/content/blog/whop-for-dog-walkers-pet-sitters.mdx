A dog walker or pet sitter sells dependable coverage at a particular time, not just access to a membership. Whop may help collect payment for a clear visit package, but the calendar and delivery record remain central. A customer must know exactly which visits they have purchased.

## Separate routine walks from holiday care

Routine weekday walks and holiday pet sitting have different capacity patterns. A regular walk may have a repeatable time window, while an overnight booking reserves a much larger block. Do not combine them under one subscription unless the included services are unambiguous.

Agree visit duration, service area, household access, pet count, and how changes are handled. Keep emergency contacts and access details in a suitable private system. This article covers service administration, not animal-health or training advice.

| Offer | What to specify |
| --- | --- |
| Individual walk | Duration, date, and collection arrangement |
| Walk package | Number of visits and booking conditions |
| Recurring plan | Included cadence and holiday exceptions |
| Pet-sitting booking | Exact dates, attendance pattern, and responsibilities |

## A ten-walk package needs ten delivery records

At an illustrative $22 per walk, a ten-walk package costs $220. Once six walks are completed, four remain. Keep that count visible in your own service ledger and make the client’s balance easy to confirm. A payment receipt alone does not decrement visits.

## Reserve capacity before promoting a holiday offer

Five customers wanting the same holiday weekend may consume all your availability even when the rest of the month is quiet. Confirm the actual dates before collecting a payment for coverage. A general product purchase limit is not a date-specific availability check.

Consider an hour-long service window containing thirty minutes of care, twenty minutes of travel, and ten minutes of updates. Four bookings occupy four hours, even though the customer-facing visit time totals two. Use total occupied time when deciding how many plans to sell.

## Make changes predictable

State how a moved walk, a canceled visit, or a holiday absence affects the package. If a visit is credited back, update the ledger. If the agreement allows a charge, show which policy applies. Do not assume checkout automatically implements every cancellation rule you write.

Test one package with an existing client and reconcile it from purchase through the last visit. A useful payment flow should make the remaining entitlement easier to understand.
