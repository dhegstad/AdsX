Big Cartel is a sensible option for an artist or maker who wants a focused shop, including a small free starting plan. Shopify is worth evaluating when your catalog, operational workflow, or integration needs justify a broader commerce setup. A bigger platform is useful only when you will use the additional capability.

This comparison uses official plan information reviewed September 9, 2026. The scenarios are our editorial decision examples, not results from testing a live merchant store.

## Compare the actual starting point

Big Cartel's [Gold plan](https://www.bigcartel.com/plans/gold) offers five free product listings and supports size and color variants. It is a practical candidate for a small collection with a limited software budget.

Its [paid-plan page](https://www.bigcartel.com/product/pricing) lists Platinum at $15 per month after its stated trial and Diamond at $30 per month. Paid plans support physical and digital products; Diamond adds functions including abandoned-cart recovery and scheduled product drops. Check the live plan for the exact features you require.

For Shopify, use the [current regional plan quote](https://www.shopify.com/pricing). Evaluate the complete storefront plan you need, along with apps and processing. Do not compare a temporary promotion with Big Cartel's ongoing price as if both lasted indefinitely.

## An artist-shop decision table

| Your situation | Starting recommendation | What could change it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Five or fewer products, modest requirements | Evaluate Big Cartel Gold | A required feature outside that plan |
| Regular product drops | Compare the relevant paid plans | Inventory, release, or integration complexity |
| Digital and physical work | Test delivery on both | Access rules, bundles, or file workflow |
| Growing operational stack | Evaluate Shopify's required apps and plan | Extra cost or integration limitations |
| A functioning shop with loyal customers | Measure the case for staying | A specific problem the current system cannot solve |

These are starting recommendations, not limits on what either platform can achieve.

## Digital products are supported on both

It is outdated to assume Big Cartel only sells physical goods. Its current paid-plan documentation includes digital products. Verify your file type, delivery requirements, and plan eligibility before choosing.

On Shopify, the free [Digital Products app](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/digital-service-product/digital-downloads) supports files and supported external links. Selling a file also requires you to define the customer's usage rights and support process.

If you sell a print with a download, test both parts of the order. Verify that the digital asset is delivered correctly and the physical item remains in the appropriate fulfillment workflow.

## Budget for your catalog's economics

A fictional illustrator earns $12 contribution per order before fixed software costs. An extra $36 of monthly software would require three additional orders to cover that difference. That calculation does not prove a platform will create those orders; it explains the amount of benefit needed.

Include the domain, apps, email service, processing, and your maintenance time. A platform with more available tools can also create more opportunities to buy software you do not need.

For a small catalog, reducing administrative effort may matter more than building a complex automation system. Track the task you want to improve before paying to improve it.

## Test the work, not a theme screenshot

Set up the same three representative products on each candidate: an ordinary item, a limited-stock variant, and the most complicated item you actually sell. Preview the product and cart on a phone.

Check whether the customer understands size, material, production time, and what is included. Then inspect the order as the person who will fulfill it. Confirm that a selected option or customization is visible without manual guesswork.

If an app or integration is essential, include it in the test and the budget. A catalog feature that works only with a paid app should be described that way in your own decision record.

## When Shopify earns the extra consideration

Shopify may fit when you need a particular verified integration, a more involved catalog operation, or a workflow that your current plan does not support efficiently. The [minimal physical-store app stack](/blog/shopify-app-stack-first-physical-store) helps keep the evaluation small.

Do not migrate just to imitate the technology used by a larger brand. Their catalog, staff, and operating constraints may have little in common with yours.

## If you already use Big Cartel

A move requires attention to old order records, product details, digital assets, and links shared on social profiles or packaging. Follow the [Big Cartel migration checklist](/blog/big-cartel-to-shopify-migration).

If the current shop works and the new platform has not demonstrated a meaningful improvement, keep selling while you evaluate. If Shopify passes your requirements at a cost you can support, make the transition with a tested catalog and clear customer destination.
