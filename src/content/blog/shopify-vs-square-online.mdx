Square is worth evaluating first when an existing retail or service business already depends on its payment and operating workflows. Shopify is worth evaluating when the online store and its commerce integrations are central to the business. For many merchants, the deciding factor is how the online and physical operations work together.

This comparison reviews official plan information as of September 9, 2026. Square prices below refer to its US page; other markets and existing agreements may differ.

## Read Square's current plans

The [Square online-store plan page](https://squareup.com/us/en/online-store/plans) lists Square Free, Plus, and Premium. It shows monthly subscription prices of $0, $49, and $149 per location, respectively. Payment processing and certain other charges remain separate.

A free software subscription does not mean every transaction or service is free. Likewise, a higher plan should be evaluated against the features and payment mix that justify it.

For Shopify, obtain the appropriate [online-store plan quote](https://www.shopify.com/pricing), then include the retail setup you require. A comparison that leaves out locations, hardware, or a POS subscription can understate the real cost of either option.

## Compare the job performed at each location

| Requirement | Evidence to collect before choosing |
| --- | --- |
| In-person checkout | Supported payment hardware and the actual staff workflow |
| Online shipping | Correct stock location and fulfillment instructions |
| Local collection | Customer availability, pickup notification, and handoff |
| Returns | Staff can locate and process the relevant order |
| Multiple locations | Stock and access rules match where work happens |
| Bookings or service sales | The chosen plan/tools support the complete customer journey |

Treat this as a test matrix for both platforms. Do not assume a feature exists in your country, on your plan, or with your hardware merely because it appears in a general promotional page.

## The cost of switching can outweigh a small subscription difference

Suppose a fictional retailer would spend $900 replacing devices and training staff. If the new setup saves $30 monthly, the simple payback is 30 months before other costs. If it also removes five hours of verified monthly manual work, the business case may be very different.

Use your own hardware quotes and measured workload. Do not assume a current Square reader works with Shopify or that a new device alone reproduces all the old operating procedures.

When comparing payment rates, split online, in-person, international, and other relevant transactions. Applying the lowest advertised rate to every sale is unlikely to reproduce the invoice.

## When Square may be the better fit

Staying with Square can make sense when the team already operates it reliably, the required online features are supported, and the catalog or fulfillment process is straightforward for that business.

A local shop that mainly needs customers to reserve or collect stocked items should test that journey before paying for a larger online stack. The ability to serve those customers reliably matters more than having unused integrations available.

Square also deserves a fresh evaluation for a new business that needs its particular combination of service, retail, and payment functions. Check the exact plan rather than relying on the name of an older Square Online tier.

## When Shopify may be the better fit

Shopify may fit when the business needs a particular ecommerce integration, a verified catalog workflow, or a more suitable connection between online merchandising and operations.

For example, an online-focused merchant may value a fulfillment integration that removes repeated order handling. Demonstrate that integration with a representative order and include the app's normal cost in the comparison.

Shopify's [plan-feature reference](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/pricing-plans/plans-features) is a starting point for matching requirements to a plan. Staff access and retail requirements should be checked explicitly; sharing a single administrator login is not a sound substitute for the necessary user access.

## Decide who owns inventory

If you plan to use Shopify online while retaining Square in person, document the synchronization arrangement before launch. Record which system controls quantities, how often data changes, and what happens when a connector fails.

A split setup can work only as well as its verified integration or deliberate stock allocation. The last unit of a product cannot be promised independently by two systems without a reconciliation plan.

Test an online order and an in-person sale of the same SKU, then inspect the quantities and the fulfillment responsibilities. Add returns to the test; a sale-only demonstration leaves part of the daily workflow unanswered.

## Make the decision with real scenarios

Run a normal sale, a pickup order, a return, and a staff handoff on the proposed setup. Record the total recurring cost and the one-time switching cost. A platform should earn the move through demonstrated improvements.
