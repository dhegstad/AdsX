Gumroad and Shopify can both support a digital-product business. Gumroad emphasizes creator selling with no monthly platform subscription and fees on sales. Shopify offers a broader store platform with a subscription, payment costs, and an app ecosystem. The right choice depends on the customer journey and operating model you need.

**Updated September 9, 2026:** This comparison corrects earlier claims about Gumroad domains and catalogs, Shopify file delivery, and a universal revenue threshold for switching. It is based on provider documentation, not a hands-on performance test.

## Compare the actual selling workflow

| Decision | Gumroad | Shopify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platform pricing model | Transaction-based; no monthly subscription in its published pricing | Subscription plus payment and any applicable app/service costs |
| Direct-sale pricing | Published headline: 10% + $0.50 per transaction | Depends on plan, market, payment method, and billing term |
| Marketplace discovery | Gumroad Discover has a separate 30% headline fee | A store does not automatically come with a marketplace audience |
| Custom domain | Supported | Supported |
| Catalog presentation | Profile and product pages with customization options | Store themes, product catalog, and supporting pages |
| Digital delivery | Built around digital-product selling | Configure a delivery app for file products |
| Operating scope | Evaluate the creator workflow you need | Evaluate the broader store and app workflow you need |

Fee figures come from [Gumroad’s pricing page](https://gumroad.com/pricing). Check the actual fee treatment and any extra services for your account before modeling take-home income. Use [Shopify’s current pricing page](https://www.shopify.com/pricing) for your market rather than treating a US example or temporary offer as universal.

## Both platforms can present a branded business

Gumroad supports custom domains through its [settings documentation](https://gumroad.com/help/article/67-the-settings-menu.html). Its [profile guide](https://gumroad.com/help/article/124-your-gumroad-profile-page) describes ways to organize and present products. It is inaccurate to describe Gumroad as having no catalog or no possibility of a branded domain.

The useful question is whether its presentation and purchasing workflow are sufficient for your business. A creator selling a few downloadable resources may value a compact setup. A merchant planning a larger store with varied product types and integrations may prefer Shopify’s broader structure.

“Ownership” is too vague to settle this comparison. Test the specific portability you need: domain control, exports, customer records, product data, and what happens when you leave. Both are hosted services with provider terms and technical constraints.

## Shopify file delivery needs configuration

You can sell digital products through Shopify, but creating a product does not automatically create a complete download-delivery system. Shopify’s [Digital Products app documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/digital-service-product/digital-downloads) explains its free delivery app. Other apps address specialized workflows; verify licensing, course access, membership, or file-handling requirements individually.

Use our [digital-product app stack](/blog/shopify-digital-products-app-stack-first-store) to map the customer journey. A PDF download and an ongoing membership are different requirements even if both businesses describe themselves as creators.

## There is no universal $500 switching threshold

Compare costs with your order count, average order value, payment mix, apps, and market. Fixed fees per transaction mean that two businesses with the same revenue can pay different amounts when their order counts differ.

For an illustration only, assume Platform A charges 10% + $0.50 per order with no base subscription. Assume Platform B costs $40 per month plus 3% + $0.30 per order. These simplified inputs are not a quote for a Shopify plan and exclude other costs.

| Monthly revenue | Orders | Platform A | Platform B |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $500 | 10 | $55 | $58 |
| $2,000 | 40 | $220 | $112 |
| $2,000 | 200 | $300 | $160 |

The formula is `fixed subscription + revenue × percentage fee + orders × fixed order fee`. Add any required apps and services before deciding. A lower modeled processing bill can be outweighed by setup or ongoing administrative work.

Gumroad states that it acts as merchant of record and handles sales-tax collection and remittance for its transactions. That is an operating distinction to investigate with your own business obligations in mind, not a reason to assume all business taxes disappear. This guide does not calculate tax liability.

## A practical decision process

List three must-have workflows and test them against the current documentation. For each platform, record whether the requirement is built in, requires another service, or is unverified. Then estimate a normal month and a busy month.

Stay with a simpler workflow when it covers the business and the cost is acceptable. Consider a broader store when the catalog, integrations, customer experience, or economics justify the additional operating work. Migration should solve an identified problem.
