Whop may help an independent lash artist collect payments for defined appointments. The key decision happens before checkout: does this client need a full set, a fill, a removal, or a consultation? A cheaper fill link should not become the default choice when the actual appointment needs more time.

## Explain who each booking is for

Describe your own fill eligibility policy in plain language. The appropriate interval and condition assessment belong to your professional practice; do not copy another artist’s rules simply to populate a booking page. Explain how work from another provider is assessed and when a different appointment may be necessary.

A client arriving for a short fill that actually requires a longer service creates a choice between running late and disappointing them. Solve that with qualification, not an unexpected charge at the chair.

| Booking type | Intake decision | Commercial detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full set | Desired service and suitability | Duration and complete quote |
| Fill | Whether existing work qualifies | Scope and rescheduling alternative |
| Removal | What work is present | Standalone fee or package treatment |
| Consultation | What can be decided remotely | Whether its fee credits a later visit |

## Show the balance before the appointment

For an illustrative $120 service with $30 paid ahead, the service balance is $90. Record the original appointment type next to that payment. If a fill changes to a full set, confirm both the new price and the available time before accepting the change.

Whop’s [checkout-link documentation](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/payment-processing/create-checkout-link) covers one-time payments and questions before purchase. That is useful for a reservation flow, but do not assume it supplies a specialist lash-client record or automatically calculates service credits.

Our [payment-link guide](/blog/whop-checkout-links-guide) helps separate what is being sold from where payment happens. For a beauty business, that distinction can prevent a deposit link from looking like a complete treatment purchase.

## Plan capacity around the longest commitment

Suppose you have six working hours after breaks and each full-set appointment occupies two hours including preparation. Three appointments use the entire window. A fourth paid booking creates an obligation you cannot meet that day even if checkout remains available.

Whop’s [Calendar Bookings app](https://whop.com/blog/calendar-bookings-app/) lets businesses configure availability. Verify the practical details—buffers, rescheduling, provider availability, and confirmation instructions—against your own workflow. Keep a dedicated beauty scheduler if it already handles constraints you cannot reproduce reliably.

## Treat memberships cautiously

A recurring plan might cover a defined number of eligible visits, but “unlimited fills” makes both capacity and client expectations harder to control. A clear menu of individual appointments may be a better first offer than a subscription.

Keep suitability records and private client information in an appropriate system. This guide concerns payment administration, not lash application or treatment advice. Confirm your actual services meet Whop’s [business eligibility rules](https://docs.whop.com/trust-and-safety/trust-safety-overview/what-is-not-allowed-on-whop).
