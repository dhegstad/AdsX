> **Quick answer:** Every Shopify plan — Basic, Grow, and Advanced — gets the same analytics dashboard, the same attribution settings, and the same core reports. What changes as you move up is report *depth*: Grow ($105/month) unlocks a larger pre-built report library, and Advanced ($399/month) adds the custom report builder. Most new stores should [start on Basic with the free trial](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624) and upgrade only when the payment-fee savings justify it — not for the reports alone.

Type "Shopify plan comparison" into any search engine and you'll get twenty pricing tables. What almost nobody documents is the question merchants actually ask before upgrading: **which reports do I get on each plan, and what am I missing?**

That gap causes expensive mistakes in both directions. Some merchants pay $399/month for Advanced because they assume Basic's analytics are crippled (they aren't). Others stay on Basic and bolt on three reporting apps to rebuild something the Grow plan already includes. This guide compares the analytics, reporting, and attribution features across Shopify Basic, Grow, and Advanced as of 2026 — and gives you the math for when upgrading beats installing an app.

For the full picture of what each tier costs beyond analytics, see our [complete Shopify pricing breakdown](/blog/shopify-pricing-2026-every-plan-real-cost).

## Shopify analytics by plan: the comparison table

Here is the per-plan analytics breakdown, based on Shopify's published plan structure as of 2026. The pattern to internalize: dashboards and attribution are universal, report *libraries* expand with each tier, and custom report building sits at the top.

| Analytics feature | Basic ($39/mo) | Grow ($105/mo) | Advanced ($399/mo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Analytics overview dashboard** (sales, sessions, conversion rate, AOV) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Live View** (real-time traffic and orders) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Finance reports** (payments, taxes, payouts) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Marketing & acquisition reports** | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Attribution model & window settings** | Yes — same on all plans | Yes | Yes |
| **Product & inventory reports** | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| **Pre-built report library** | Core set | Expanded set (fuller sales, profit & customer reporting) | Full library |
| **Customer cohort / retention depth** | Basic breakdowns | Deeper customer reports | Deepest |
| **Customize & save report variations** | Limited | Mid-tier subset | Full |
| **Custom report builder** (build from scratch) | No | No | Yes |
| **ShopifyQL-powered exploration** | No | No | Yes (also Plus) |
| **Online card rate** (Shopify Payments, US) | 2.9% + 30¢ | 2.7% + 30¢ | 2.5% + 30¢ |

Two things in that table surprise most merchants: attribution settings are not plan-gated, and the analytics dashboard everyone screenshots in tutorials is identical on a $39 plan and a $399 plan.

## What analytics do all Shopify plans include?

Every Shopify plan from Basic up includes the same core analytics as of 2026: the overview dashboard (total sales, sessions, online store conversion rate, average order value, returning customer rate, sales by channel, top products), Live View for real-time monitoring, finance reports for payouts and taxes, product and inventory reporting, and the marketing reports that show sessions and sales attributed to each channel and campaign. None of that is an upsell.

In practice, the overview dashboard answers the questions a store owner asks daily — is revenue up, where is traffic coming from, what's converting — and it answers them the same way on Basic as on Advanced. During your [first 30 days on Shopify](/blog/first-30-days-shopify-store-checklist), you will live in this dashboard regardless of plan, and nothing about being on Basic will hold you back.

The universal tier also includes UTM-aware acquisition reporting: tag your ad and email links properly, and Basic's marketing reports will break down sessions, orders, and sales by source, medium, and campaign. For a store running paid traffic, that is the minimum viable measurement stack — and it costs nothing extra.

## What reports does Grow add over Basic?

The Grow plan ($105/month) expands the pre-built report library rather than changing the analytics experience: historically, the mid-tier plan is where Shopify's fuller sales, profit, and customer reporting unlocks — richer breakdowns like detailed sales by dimension, margin-aware reporting, and deeper customer segmentation than Basic's core set. Grow also permits more customization of existing reports (adjusting filters and columns and saving variations), while stopping short of Advanced's build-from-scratch report builder.

The honest caveat: Shopify has reshuffled its analytics several times, most recently in its major analytics overhaul, and the exact list of which pre-built reports sit at which tier shifts. Before upgrading *specifically* for one report, open the Analytics section of your admin and check whether the report you want shows a plan-upgrade prompt — Shopify marks gated reports directly in the interface, which is more reliable than any third-party table (including this one).

Where Grow's reporting genuinely earns its price is customer analysis. If your business depends on repeat purchase behavior — subscriptions, consumables, anything where retention drives the model — Basic's customer breakdowns get thin, and the deeper customer and cohort-style reporting on higher tiers starts mattering. If retention cohorts are central to how you run the business, verify cohort report availability in your admin on your current plan before assuming an upgrade fixes it.

## What does Advanced actually add for analytics?

Advanced ($399/month) adds the one reporting capability no lower plan has: the custom report builder. Instead of choosing from pre-built reports, you build your own — pick the dimensions, metrics, filters, and breakdowns, then save the result as a report your team can rerun. As of 2026, this is the single biggest analytics difference between plans, and it has historically been gated to Advanced and Plus.

Advanced is also the tier where ShopifyQL-powered exploration lives. ShopifyQL is Shopify's SQL-like query language for commerce data; Shopify retired the standalone Notebooks experience during its analytics overhaul, and what remains is the querying depth behind custom reports on higher plans plus developer API access. If you were hoping to write ad-hoc queries against your store data on Basic, that is not on the menu — export to a spreadsheet or BI tool instead.

Be clear-eyed about what Advanced does *not* add: no extra attribution features, no different dashboard, no data that Basic isn't already collecting. Your store records the same events on every plan; Advanced changes how flexibly you can query and present them. The rest of Advanced's value sits outside analytics entirely — lower card rates, lower third-party gateway fees, and features like duties collection. ## Is Shopify attribution different on Basic vs Advanced?

No — and this is the most persistent myth in Shopify plan comparisons. As of 2026, Shopify's marketing attribution works identically on Basic, Grow, and Advanced: the same click-based model options, the same default (a last-non-direct-click style model with roughly a 30-day lookback), and the same ability to switch models and windows inside the marketing reports. Upgrading your plan does not change how Shopify decides which channel gets credit for a sale.

What upgrading changes is your ability to *interrogate* attributed data — slicing attributed revenue by product line, discount usage, or customer type in a custom report is an Advanced capability. But the attribution itself, the part merchants think they're buying, is universal.

That matters because Shopify's click-based attribution has the same blind spots on every plan: it undercounts channels that drive delayed or cross-device purchases (TikTok, influencer, podcast, YouTube) and disagrees with what Meta and Google claim in their own dashboards. No plan upgrade fixes that — the discrepancy is inherent to click-based measurement. The practical fix that works on any plan is asking customers directly: a post-purchase survey app like [OrderSurvey](https://apps.shopify.com/ordersurvey) adds a "How did you hear about us?" question to the order confirmation flow, giving you zero-party attribution data that click models miss. We compared the options in our [post-purchase survey apps guide](/blog/best-shopify-post-purchase-survey-apps).

So if better attribution is your reason for eyeing Advanced, redirect the budget: keep the cheaper plan, add a survey app, and spend the difference on traffic.

## When is Basic's reporting enough?

Basic's analytics are enough for most stores doing under roughly $30–50K/month: the dashboard covers daily performance, marketing reports cover channel and campaign results if you tag links with UTMs, and finance reports cover the accounting. If you can answer "what's selling, what's converting, and which channel drove it" from the admin — and on Basic, you can — you don't have a reporting problem yet.

Basic starts to pinch when the questions get more specific than the pre-built reports allow. Warning signs you've outgrown it:

1. **You export to spreadsheets weekly** to rebuild the same view Shopify won't save for you
2. **Margin questions go unanswered** — you know revenue by product but not profitability by product
3. **Retention is the business model** and you need cohort-level repeat-purchase visibility
4. **Multiple people need different saved views** — founder, media buyer, ops — and everyone's re-filtering the same reports

Even then, the next step usually isn't Advanced. It's either Grow (if a gated pre-built report is what you're missing) or an app (if you need flexibility no plan provides). And if you're pre-launch, none of this should slow you down: [start the free trial on Basic](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624) — currently free to try, then $1/month for three months — and let real data tell you which reports you actually miss.

## Should you upgrade to Advanced or install an analytics app?

The clean way to decide: never upgrade to Advanced for analytics alone — upgrade when the payment-fee savings cover the plan cost, and take the custom report builder as a free bonus. Advanced's real financial lever is its card rate (2.5% + 30¢ vs Grow's 2.7% + 30¢ and Basic's 2.9% + 30¢), plus a lower third-party gateway surcharge if you don't use Shopify Payments. We break down the full fee structure in our [Shopify transaction fees guide](/blog/shopify-transaction-credit-card-fees-explained).

Here's the approximate breakeven math on 2026 pricing:

| Upgrade path | Extra plan cost | Card-rate savings | Fee-savings breakeven (monthly card sales) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic → Grow | +$66/mo | 0.2 points | ~$33,000/mo |
| Grow → Advanced | +$294/mo | 0.2 points | ~$147,000/mo |
| Basic → Advanced | +$360/mo | 0.5 points | ~$72,000/mo |

Below those volumes, the upgrade costs more than it saves, which means you'd be paying the difference purely for reports. Compare that to the app route: reporting and analytics apps on the Shopify App Store — the better-known options run from ~$20–100/month depending on store size — offer custom report building, profit analytics, and scheduled exports that in many cases exceed what Advanced's native builder does. On pure reporting flexibility per dollar, apps win below the breakeven line; we've covered how the measurement stack fits together in [Shopify apps that improve ROAS](/blog/shopify-apps-that-improve-roas).

Above the breakeven line, the logic flips: Advanced effectively pays for itself in processing fees, native reports keep your data inside the admin with no extra vendor, and you can drop an app subscription or two. That's also the point where analytics stops being the deciding factor and total cost takes over — model it alongside everything else in our [true first-year cost breakdown](/blog/true-cost-running-shopify-store-first-year).

The one exception worth naming: if a single gated report would change a decision you make every week — say, margin by product for a catalog with wildly varying COGS — paying for Grow early is rational even before any breakeven math. Reports are only expensive until one of them saves you from a bad decision.

## Bottom line

Shopify's plans differ far less on analytics than the pricing page implies. Basic, Grow, and Advanced share the same dashboard, the same data collection, and the same attribution; Grow buys you a deeper pre-built report library, and Advanced buys you the custom report builder plus ShopifyQL-level depth. Attribution — the feature people most often upgrade hoping to improve — is identical everywhere, and its click-model blind spots are better patched with a post-purchase survey than a plan change.

So sequence it like this: [start on Basic](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624) (free trial, then $1/month for three months), tag your links, add a survey app for attribution truth, and upgrade only when either a specific gated report starts costing you decisions or your card volume crosses the breakeven line where Advanced pays for itself. That way the analytics you're paying for are always the analytics you're actually using.
