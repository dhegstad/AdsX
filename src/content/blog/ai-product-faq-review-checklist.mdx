AI can help organize customer questions and draft answers. The facts still need to come from your product records, current policies, and people who understand the offer. A useful product FAQ resolves a buying question with an answer you can verify.

Do not begin by generating twenty generic questions for every product. Start with actual support questions, product-page confusion, and the details a customer needs to choose the correct variant.

## Build a small evidence sheet

| Customer question | Source of truth | Publish when |
|---|---|---|
| Will it fit my device? | Verified compatibility list | The exact model or limitation is known |
| What comes in the box? | Current packing specification | The list matches the sold variant |
| Can it go in the dishwasher? | Supplier or manufacturer care instructions | The claim is supported |
| When will it arrive? | Current fulfillment and delivery policy | The answer reflects location and cutoff limits |
| Can I use the template commercially? | Applicable license | The permitted use is clearly stated |
| Is this color available? | Current product availability | The answer will not become a stale stock promise |

Keep volatile answers close to a maintained source. A static FAQ saying “all colors are in stock” can become wrong immediately after a sale.

## Give AI a constrained drafting task

Use a prompt such as: “Draft short answers to these customer questions using only the supplied fact sheet. Identify the source for each answer in a separate review table. Mark missing information as an unresolved question. Do not add guarantees, certifications, or delivery promises.”

This is an original example prompt, not a guarantee that any model will follow every constraint. Review the output. Shopify's [AI product description guidance](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/details/product-descriptions/shopify-magic) is useful background for working with generated product text. Our [Magic and Sidekick workflow guide](/blog/shopify-magic-sidekick-ai-features-2026) explains how to bound a store task.

## Review answers in two passes

First check truth: numbers, dimensions, materials, compatibility, included accessories, policies, and exceptions. Trace every factual statement to the evidence sheet. Remove unsupported certainty instead of softening it with “usually” when you still do not know the answer.

Then check usefulness. Does the first sentence answer the question? Does the customer need a link to a size chart or specification? Does the answer distinguish between variants? If an exception is essential to the decision, keep it beside the main answer rather than burying it below unrelated copy.

Here is a hypothetical edit:

- Weak draft: “Our organizer fits every desk and keeps your workspace perfectly tidy.”
- Reviewed answer: “The organizer is 28 cm wide and 12 cm deep. Measure your available space before ordering. Cables and stationery are not included.”

The reviewed answer is useful only if those dimensions and exclusions are verified for the actual product.

## Publish answers customers can find

Place the answer near the relevant product information. Use clear headings and readable text. Check mobile display and make sure the content is available without a broken interaction or hidden account requirement.

Google's [guidance for AI features in Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says the usual search fundamentals continue to apply; special AI markup is not required. A FAQ does not guarantee an AI citation or a search result enhancement. Its first job is to help someone understand the product.

## Maintain the answers after launch

Record the product identifier, question, evidence source, reviewer, and review date. Recheck answers when a supplier specification, product bundle, shipping policy, or license changes. Monitor new customer questions for gaps.

For an initial pilot, choose five products with repeated support questions. Compare the types of questions received after the update, while noting changes in traffic or sales volume. A smaller number of messages is useful context, not proof of a conversion lift.
