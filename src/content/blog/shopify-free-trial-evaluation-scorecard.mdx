Use a Shopify trial to answer whether the platform fits your business. Creating an account is only the beginning. A useful evaluation ends with evidence that you can present your products, accept the relevant payments, deliver orders, and operate at an affordable ongoing cost.

Trial terms and offers can change. Check [Shopify’s current trial page](https://www.shopify.com/free-trial) and the [pricing page for your market](https://www.shopify.com/pricing) before planning around a specific duration or promotion.

## Define your must-pass requirements

Choose a representative product and a typical customer order. Include an unusual requirement if it is central to the business: made-to-order lead times, digital delivery, subscriptions, local collection, or an external fulfillment partner.

Avoid spending the entire evaluation choosing colors and fonts. Store design matters, but it cannot settle a missing delivery capability or an unaffordable operating model.

## Score the workflow with evidence

| Area | Question | Evidence to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | Can the actual item and its options be represented clearly? | A representative product preview |
| Checkout | Do the required market and payment arrangements work? | Documented eligibility and a suitable test |
| Delivery | Can the order reach the customer correctly? | Fulfillment steps or digital delivery verification |
| Operations | Can the responsible people carry out their tasks? | Plan access and workflow review |
| Budget | Does the store work financially at normal pricing? | Ongoing cost worksheet |
| Customer help | Can a buyer find policies and assistance? | Customer-facing page review |

Use “passed,” “failed,” and “unverified” rather than an arbitrary numeric score. One failed must-have can matter more than five successful cosmetic checks.

## Verify the requirements that are easy to overlook

The [official new-store checklist](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/new-to-shopify-checklists/general-checklist) covers setup, testing, and launch. It also explains that launching an online store during a trial requires choosing a subscription plan. A storefront preview is therefore not the same milestone as a public store accepting customers.

Record requirements involving your country, product category, team, and sales channels. Check the relevant provider documentation rather than assuming an app or payment method works everywhere because it appears in a tutorial.

If your workflow needs an app, include its configuration and cost in the evaluation. Read the [minimal physical-store stack](/blog/shopify-app-stack-first-physical-store) or [digital-product stack](/blog/shopify-digital-products-app-stack-first-store) to scope that work.

## Separate setup cost from ongoing cost

Build three rows: initial setup, promotional period, and normal operations. Include the platform plan, apps, domain, payment fees, and external services. Add the time needed to maintain the process if it replaces a simpler selling method.

A fictional store with $80 of fixed monthly software costs and $16 contribution per order needs five orders to cover those software costs. It still needs to cover the other business expenses. This is a break-even illustration, not a Shopify price or a sales forecast.

## Make a clear decision at the end

Choose among three outcomes: proceed because the requirements are verified; continue research on a specific unresolved requirement; or select another approach because a must-have fails.

If you proceed, keep the scorecard for your launch checklist. If you stop, record why. That makes future platform comparisons easier and prevents a promotional deadline from replacing a business decision.
