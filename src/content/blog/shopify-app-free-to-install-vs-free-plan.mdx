“Free to install” means an app can be installed without an upfront installation charge. It does not tell you the total cost of using it. A free plan may have limits, and a free trial may turn into a paid subscription. Read the pricing details before treating any label as a budget.

Shopify explains app billing in its [app charges documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/manage-billing/billing-charges/types-of-charges/third-party-charges/app-charges). Charges can include subscriptions, usage, and one-time purchases. Some services also bill externally.

## Translate the label into a cost question

| Label | What to establish before relying on it |
| --- | --- |
| Free | Does the required workflow work without a paid upgrade or external service? |
| Free plan available | Which features and usage limits apply to that plan? |
| Free to install | What event creates a charge after installation? |
| Free trial | When does the trial end, and what billing approval is required? |
| Usage-based | What unit is billed, what is included, and how is the cap handled? |

This table is a buyer’s checklist. The app’s current listing and billing approval determine the actual offer. A free email app, for example, may be useful for a small list but have a different cost once the number of contacts or sends grows.

## Calculate a normal month and a busy month

Imagine two fictional apps that solve the same job.

**App A:** $29 per month, including 500 billable events, then $0.05 for each additional event.

**App B:** No base fee, with a $0.08 charge for every billable event.

| Monthly events | App A | App B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | $29 | $8 |
| 500 | $29 | $40 |
| 2,000 | $104 | $160 |

The free-to-start option is cheaper at low usage in this example, but not at higher usage. These are invented prices, not app recommendations. Real comparisons must also check which events count: all orders, only attributed orders, successful sends, or something else.

Write down your expected usage and a realistic busy-month figure. If the unit is unfamiliar, ask the vendor for an example invoice using your numbers.

## Check costs outside the headline app fee

An app may depend on another paid account, charge for a service it arranges, or require an upgrade to access the one feature you need. Some costs may be billed outside Shopify. Ask how to cancel those separately rather than assuming uninstalling the Shopify app closes every connected subscription.

Include setup work, migration, and the cost of leaving. A tool that is technically free can still create a large switching task if customer records or reports cannot be exported in a useful format.

Shopify’s [finding and choosing apps guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/apps/finding-choosing-apps) provides the official starting point for reviewing listings. For data access, use our [app permissions checklist](/blog/shopify-app-permissions-checklist-merchants).

## Know what happens when usage grows

A usage cap is not the same as a guarantee that the app will keep doing its job at no additional cost. Determine whether hitting a limit stops the workflow, prompts approval, reduces features, or requires a plan change.

Ask three concrete questions: “What would my bill be at this volume?”, “What happens when I reach the limit?”, and “Where can I see current usage?” Keep the answers with the installation record.

App subscription cycles can differ from the store’s Shopify billing cycle. Compare activity periods before treating two invoices as duplicate billing. Uninstalling can stop future recurring charges while leaving charges already incurred payable; consult the billing documentation for the relevant charge type.

## Keep a one-line register for every installed app

Record the app name, job, owner, plan, billing unit, normal-month estimate, trial end date, external accounts, and cancellation steps. Set a review date before the trial decision is due. This turns a collection of installations into a budget you can explain.
