A Shopify app audit should explain what each tool does, what makes its bill change, and what happens if you remove it. Counting installed apps alone does not answer those questions. A store with five poorly understood subscriptions can have more waste than one with fifteen well-managed tools.

Start with invoices and operating requirements. This worksheet is for an existing merchant reviewing an app stack; a first-time merchant can use the [minimal physical-store stack](/blog/shopify-app-stack-first-physical-store) to avoid unnecessary purchases at launch.

## Build a register that matches the bills

| Field | What to record | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| App and owner | Tool name and responsible person | Someone must approve renewal |
| Job | The specific workflow it supports | Reveals overlapping tools |
| Billing source | Shopify invoice or vendor invoice | Prevents missing external subscriptions |
| Fixed commitment | Amount, currency, billing cycle | Shows minimum ongoing cost |
| Usage rule | Orders, contacts, messages, revenue, or other unit | Explains growth in charges |
| Next renewal | Actual date | Makes cancellation review actionable |
| Removal dependency | Data, theme block, customer workflow | Prevents an expensive breakage |

Shopify's [app-charge documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/manage-billing/billing-charges/types-of-charges/third-party-charges/app-charges) explains separate app billing cycles and external billing. Uninstalling an app does not necessarily cancel a subscription billed directly by its developer.

## Calculate the cost of the job

Group tools by their operating role: collecting reviews, fulfilling downloads, managing returns, sending messages, or reporting results. Then total all spending associated with that role. Two apps can appear different on the bill while duplicating the same customer-facing widget.

In an illustrative review, a store has a $20 review app, a $15 widget tool, and a $25 retention app. If the merchant uses only one overlapping display feature in all three, the useful comparison is the $60 spent on that job. It is not a claim that any named app costs these amounts.

Compare the job with [Shopify's built-in features](/blog/shopify-built-in-features-vs-apps). Retain an app when it supplies a requirement you actually use or produces a measurable operating benefit.

## Model the next usage band

Ask what the bill would be at twice the current order count, with twice the contact list, or during a seasonal peak. Do not assume the charging unit is identical to the number of orders shown in your store analytics. An app may count imported records, attempted sends, or another documented unit.

Write down the included allowance, overage price, spending cap, and behavior at the cap. A cap that stops billing may also stop a workflow; obtain the vendor's answer before relying on it during a launch. The [merchant guide to app usage limits](/blog/shopify-app-usage-charges-spending-limits) provides a worked example.

## Compare savings with the cost of removal

For each candidate, record an alternative, migration work, data export requirements, and rollback steps. A hypothetical $30 monthly saving is $360 over twelve modeled months. If replacing the tool takes substantial paid development and adds operational risk, a controlled migration may be more sensible than an immediate cancellation.

Inspect [permissions](/blog/shopify-app-permissions-checklist-merchants) and ownership before removing an integration. Check whether scheduled jobs, theme blocks, or customer communications depend on it. Follow Shopify's [app management guidance](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/apps) and the vendor's removal instructions.

## Close the audit with evidence

Keep three decisions: retain, investigate, or remove on a named date. For a removed app, save the cancellation confirmation and compare the next bill with the expected result. A lower installed-app count is not proof of lower spending.

Review the register when an app changes price, when order volume changes materially, or when a new team member proposes another tool. The useful outcome is a smaller set of unexplained charges and a clear owner for every recurring commitment.
