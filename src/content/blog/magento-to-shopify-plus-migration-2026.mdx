> **Quick answer:** Migrating from Magento to Shopify Plus trades servers, security patches, version-upgrade projects, and agency retainers for one flat SaaS subscription — and for most merchants, total cost of ownership drops even though [Shopify Plus](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624?u=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.shopify.com%2Fplus) starts around $2,300–2,500/month in 2026. Run the migration in this order: data audit → catalog → customers → orders → URL redirects → integrations → B2B → cutover. Simple catalogs move in 4–8 weeks; complex B2B builds take 4–6 months.

If you run a store on Magento — Open Source or Adobe Commerce — you have probably had the replatforming conversation at least once this year. This guide is written for the person who has to make that call: the merchant, e-commerce director, or IT lead weighing whether the move to Shopify Plus is worth it, what it actually costs, what breaks, and how long it takes. It is not an implementation tutorial for the developer doing the data mapping; it is the playbook for deciding and scoping.

## Why is 2026 a Magento migration wave?

The short version: the cost of *staying* on Magento keeps rising while the cost of leaving keeps falling. Magento was built for an era when serious e-commerce meant owning your stack — your servers, your code, your patch schedule. In 2026, most merchants have concluded that none of that ownership shows up in revenue, and all of it shows up in invoices.

Four pressures are pushing merchants off Magento right now:

1. **The patch treadmill never stops.** Adobe ships security patches on a regular cadence, and applying them is not optional — unpatched Magento stores are a favorite target for card-skimming attacks. Every patch is developer time; every delayed patch is risk you carry personally.
2. **Version upgrades are projects, not updates.** Major Magento version upgrades routinely break custom modules and third-party extensions, which is why they are typically scoped as agency projects with their own budgets. Merchants who lived through the Magento 1 end-of-life in 2020 remember what a forced re-platforming feels like.
3. **Adobe's roadmap keeps moving.** Adobe's focus has shifted toward its cloud-service version of Commerce, which for self-hosted merchants reads as yet another migration decision looming — except this one keeps you inside the same cost structure.
4. **The talent pool is thinning.** Experienced Magento developers are getting scarcer and more expensive as agencies pivot their practices toward SaaS platforms. Scarcity shows up directly in your retainer.

None of this means Magento stopped working. It means the total cost of keeping it working has become the largest hidden line item in many merchants' P&L — which is exactly the comparison to run next.

## Magento vs Shopify Plus: what does each actually cost to run?

The honest comparison is not license price versus subscription price — it is everything you pay to keep a Magento store secure, fast, and current, versus a flat subscription that includes all of it. Here is the total-cost-of-ownership picture in 2026:

| Cost line | Magento / Adobe Commerce | Shopify Plus |
|---|---|---|
| **Platform license** | Open Source: free license · Adobe Commerce: significant annual license that scales with revenue | Subscription from ~$2,300–2,500/month |
| **Hosting & infrastructure** | Yours to buy and size: servers or cloud, CDN, staging environments, traffic-spike headroom | Included — fully managed, scales automatically |
| **DevOps / sysadmin** | Ongoing: someone deploys, monitors, and maintains environments | Not required |
| **Security patching** | Mandatory recurring developer work, on Adobe's schedule | Handled by Shopify, invisibly |
| **Version upgrades** | Periodic major projects that can break custom modules | Continuous and automatic |
| **Agency retainer** | Near-universal, largely consumed by maintenance | Optional — spent on growth work, not upkeep |
| **Checkout** | Built and maintained by you/your agency | Shop Pay + Checkout Extensibility included |
| **Extensions** | Marketplace modules, each one a patch-cycle liability | 8,000+ apps, vendor-maintained |

Two honest caveats on that table. First, Shopify Plus is not cheap in absolute terms — if your revenue does not justify Plus-tier pricing, a standard Shopify plan may fit better, and we have broken down [what every Shopify plan really costs in 2026](/blog/shopify-pricing-2026-every-plan-real-cost) as well as [when Plus beats Advanced](/blog/shopify-plus-vs-advanced-when-to-upgrade). Second, SaaS does not mean cost-free: apps, themes, and transaction economics still add up, and it pays to know the [hidden Shopify costs before you commit](/blog/hidden-shopify-costs-how-to-avoid).

But the structural difference holds: on Magento, a large share of your annual spend buys *standing still* — patches, upgrades, hosting, monitoring. On Plus, the equivalent spend is either eliminated or redirected toward things that grow revenue. If you want to sanity-check the platform before scoping anything with an agency, [start a free Shopify trial](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624) and spend an hour in the admin — the contrast with the Magento back office is itself useful diligence data.

## What do you lose leaving Magento?

Be clear-eyed about this, because the losses are real — they are just losses most merchants discover they never needed. Magento's superpower is that you can change *anything*; Shopify's constraint is that you customize within guardrails.

- **Root-level control.** No server access, no custom PHP in the request path, no database queries against your own schema. If a compliance or data-residency requirement mandates self-hosting, that alone can end the conversation.
- **Unlimited catalog modeling.** Magento's attribute system lets you model almost any product structure. Shopify's model is simpler — options, variants (up to 2,048 per product as of 2026 under the newer product model), and metafields. Most catalogs map cleanly; the most exotic configurable-product setups need rethinking.
- **Total checkout control.** Magento lets you rebuild checkout from the ground up. On Plus you customize through Checkout Extensibility and Shopify Functions — powerful, but within Shopify's framework, not around it.
- **Native multi-store depth.** Magento's multi-website/multi-store hierarchy is genuinely flexible. Plus offers multiple expansion stores and strong multi-currency/multi-language tooling, but merchants running elaborate shared-catalog store trees will need to redesign that architecture.

If two or more of those are core to how your business operates — not how it *could* operate, how it actually does — weigh the migration much more carefully.

## What do you gain on Shopify Plus?

The gains cluster around three things: a checkout that converts better, an ecosystem that moves faster than any single agency, and the permanent deletion of the maintenance workstream.

- **Checkout conversion.** Shop Pay gives buyers one-tap checkout with payment details saved across every Shopify store, and Shopify publishes that it lifts conversion versus guest checkout. Self-hosted platforms simply cannot replicate a shared accelerated-checkout network. Plus adds Checkout Extensibility for upsells, custom fields, and validation logic — and positions you for where checkout is heading next, including [agentic and AI-assisted checkout flows](/blog/agentic-checkout-optimization-shopify).
- **The app ecosystem.** Over 8,000 apps replace what Magento does with custom modules — reviews, search, subscriptions, loyalty, ERP connectors — and the vendor maintains each one, not your retainer.
- **No patch cycle, ever.** Security, upgrades, scaling, PCI compliance: Shopify's problem. Your developers ship growth work instead of maintenance work. Merchants consistently report this as the change they feel most six months post-migration.
- **B2B included.** B2B on Shopify ships with Plus: company profiles, per-customer price lists, payment terms, vaulted cards, and quantity rules — no separate module license.
- **Predictable spend.** One line item you can put in front of a CFO, instead of a hosting bill, a license invoice, a retainer, and a surprise upgrade project.

## The migration sequence, step by step

Every successful Magento-to-Shopify migration follows the same order of operations: audit, catalog, customers, orders, redirects, integrations, B2B, build, cutover. The failures skip steps — almost always redirects or integration mapping.

### 1. Audit and scope the data

Before quoting anything, inventory what you actually have: SKU count, attribute count, category tree depth, customer records, order history volume, active extensions, and every system Magento talks to (ERP, PIM, WMS, email, reviews, search). Half of your Magento extensions typically exist to fix Magento problems — mark those for deletion, not migration.

### 2. Migrate the catalog

Products, variants, images, and pricing move via migration apps or CSV/API imports. The real work is mapping: Magento categories become Shopify collections, custom attributes become metafields, and configurable products become products with variants. Do a sample import of your 50 most complex products first — if those survive, the rest of the catalog will.

### 3. Migrate customers

Customer records move; passwords do not — hashing schemes are incompatible on every platform migration, not just this one. Plan an account-invite or password-reset campaign as part of launch communications, and migrate customer tags and groups so your segmentation survives.

### 4. Bring order history

Import historical orders so support staff can see purchase history and customers can see past orders. Decide how far back matters — many merchants import 2–3 years into Shopify and archive the rest in a warehouse or BI tool.

### 5. Build the redirect map (this is the SEO step)

Shopify enforces its own URL structure — /products/, /collections/, /pages/ — so **every Magento URL changes**. Export every indexed URL, map each to its new home, and load 301 redirects via Shopify's bulk CSV import before cutover. Cover category pages, old CMS pages, and blog paths, not just products. This discipline is identical at every scale — it is the same step that makes or breaks an [Etsy migration](/blog/etsy-to-shopify-migration-guide) or a [Squarespace move](/blog/migrate-squarespace-to-shopify-guide) — but the enterprise version involves thousands of URLs instead of dozens, so script it, don't hand-build it.

### 6. Map apps and integrations

For each Magento extension and integration, decide: replace with a Shopify app, replace with a native feature, rebuild via API, or retire. ERP and email-platform connectors deserve the most testing time — they are where mid-project surprises live.

### 7. Rebuild B2B

Map Magento company accounts, shared catalogs, and negotiated pricing onto B2B on Shopify's company profiles, price lists, and payment terms. Gaps (complex approval workflows, bespoke quoting) get an app or custom build — identify them in the audit, not in week ten.

### 8. Theme, test, cut over

Build the storefront, run end-to-end test orders (including B2B terms and tax edge cases), freeze Magento data changes, do a final delta sync of orders and customers, verify redirects on staging, and flip DNS in a low-traffic window.

## How long does a Magento to Shopify Plus migration take?

Timeline is driven by integration count and B2B complexity far more than by SKU count. As of 2026, realistic ranges look like this:

| Complexity | Typical profile | Realistic timeline |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | Under ~1,000 SKUs, standard attributes, few integrations, B2C only | 4–8 weeks |
| **Mid** | 1,000–20,000 SKUs, ERP or email integrations, custom attributes, light B2B | 2–4 months |
| **Complex** | 20,000+ SKUs, full B2B price lists, multi-store, ERP/PIM/WMS integrations | 4–6+ months |

Add buffer for the freeze-and-cutover window and for post-launch redirect monitoring. And be suspicious of any quote that promises a complex build in six weeks — comparing agency case studies and merchant post-mortems, blown timelines almost always trace back to underscoped integrations, not slow data transfer.

## When should you stay on Magento?

Migration is the right call for most Magento merchants in 2026, but not all of them — and the exceptions are worth naming plainly. Stay put, at least for now, if:

- **You have a hard self-hosting requirement.** Regulatory, data-residency, or corporate-policy constraints that mandate owning the infrastructure are disqualifying for any SaaS platform.
- **Your business logic genuinely lives in custom code.** If your checkout, pricing, or catalog behavior depends on deep customizations that took years to build and truly differentiate you, rebuilding inside Shopify's guardrails may cost more than your current maintenance.
- **You just signed a multi-year Adobe Commerce contract.** The math changes when the license is sunk; revisit at renewal.
- **You have cheap, loyal in-house Magento talent.** A lean Open Source setup with salaried developers who like the work can undercut Plus pricing — as long as those people stay.

If none of those describe you, the burden of proof has flipped: the question is no longer "why leave Magento?" but "what exactly are we paying all this maintenance for?"

## Bottom line

The Magento-to-Shopify-Plus migration is fundamentally a trade: you give up root access and unlimited customization, and you get back a converting checkout, a vendor-maintained app ecosystem, and every hour and dollar you currently spend on patches, upgrades, and hosting. In 2026, with Magento maintenance costs rising and Shopify's enterprise feature set (B2B, Checkout Extensibility, expansion stores) filling in its old gaps, that trade favors migration for most merchants — which is exactly why the wave is happening.

Run the process in order: audit your data and integrations, price the true Magento run-rate against a Plus subscription, and scope the migration against your real complexity tier — not an optimistic one. If you are earlier in that journey, [start a free Shopify trial](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624), rebuild five of your hardest products and one B2B price list as a proof of concept, and let the platform make its own case before any contract gets signed.
