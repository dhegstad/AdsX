Whop can be evaluated as a payment tool for a pressure-washing business once the job has been assessed and quoted. It should not turn every driveway, facade, or deck into the same fixed-price purchase. Surface type, access, condition, and the agreed result determine what you can responsibly offer.

## Use an enquiry before a broad fixed-price checkout

A customer saying “wash the house” may include gutters, windows, a detached garage, and a patio in that phrase. Your quote should name the surfaces you will work on and the areas you will leave alone. Technical methods and suitability decisions belong to qualified operators; this guide covers commercial organization.

Ask for the service address, approximate area, photos when useful, and access constraints through an appropriate intake channel. Inspect when the information is insufficient. Do not let a price link imply that a photo guarantees a complete assessment.

| Quote component | Why it changes the agreement |
| --- | --- |
| Named surfaces | Prevents a driveway quote becoming a whole-property promise |
| Material and condition | Determines whether you can accept the requested job |
| Access and utilities | Affects setup and site readiness |
| Exclusions | Separates stain expectations and unrelated work |
| Weather arrangement | Explains how dates move when work cannot proceed |

## Tie the payment to a version of the quote

For an illustrative $480 job, a $120 agreed reservation payment leaves $360 toward the service price. Keep the accepted quote and both payment records under one job reference. If the owner adds a patio later, agree a revised total before sending a new link.

Whop’s [checkout-link guide](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/payment-processing/create-checkout-link) documents custom one-time links. An [invoice](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/manage-payments/send-invoice) is another possible collection route, but preview its total: the current invoice guide describes a 5% service fee paid by the customer. Quote and checkout must tell a consistent price story.

Our [invoice guide](/blog/whop-invoices-guide) explains the difference between asking for payment and confirming it has arrived. Do not mark a job paid because an invoice was merely sent.

## Plan for weather without inventing automation

Write a simple process for moving a job: who contacts the customer, when a replacement date is confirmed, and whether the existing reservation payment carries forward. Follow the applicable agreement and consumer rules for refunds; a checkout label does not settle those questions by itself.

Keep your field calendar and routing process unless a replacement has been tested. A Whop payment does not establish weather-aware scheduling, crew availability, or equipment readiness.

## Record completion against the agreed scope

A private completion record should show the work performed and any agreed exceptions. Obtain permission before using property photos publicly. Before-and-after images can support a conversation, but they should not be edited to imply results you did not achieve.

Compare estimated and actual hours across a pilot group of jobs. A four-hour site visit with an hour of travel occupies five hours, so a $480 example produces $96 gross per occupied hour before equipment, fuel, labor, fees, and other costs.
