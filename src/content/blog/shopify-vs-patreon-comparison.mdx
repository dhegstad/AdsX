Choose Patreon when the central offer is an ongoing relationship with a creator and an integrated membership experience. Consider Shopify when the central offer is a product catalog and a branded commerce operation. Creators who sell both memberships and merchandise may have a reason to use both, provided they can explain and support the separate experiences.

Neither choice creates an audience for you. Compare the purchase and delivery workflow your existing or prospective customers actually need.

## Match the platform to the offer

| Decision | Patreon | Shopify |
|---|---|---|
| Recurring creator content | Membership is a central product | Design an access and subscription workflow |
| Physical product catalog | Check the fit of available commerce features | Catalog, checkout, inventory, and fulfillment are central |
| Digital downloads | One-time sales are available | Add the delivery workflow your product requires |
| Customer experience | A Patreon creator page and member experience | Your own storefront, with chosen apps where needed |
| Operating cost | Platform and other applicable fees | Plan, payments, apps, and operating costs |

Patreon's [pricing page](https://www.patreon.com/pricing) describes its membership, community, and commerce offering. Shopify's [digital product documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/digital-service-product) explains the delivery options for a store. A content membership, a downloadable file, and a recurring shipment are different products even when all three charge monthly.

## Compare the complete fee stack

Patreon's current standard platform plan charges 10%, with other applicable charges. Legacy creators can have different terms. Payment processing, currency conversion, payout, taxes on fees, and eligible iOS purchases can change the total. Check your actual account against the [creator fee overview](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/11111747095181-Creator-fees-overview).

For Shopify, obtain the plan quote for your location, the payment-provider rates, and the apps needed to deliver the promised experience. Our [Shopify cost guide](/blog/hidden-shopify-costs-how-to-avoid) separates these categories. A store with paid membership software should include that subscription before making a comparison.

Use a worksheet with one row per charge. Mark the base to which each percentage applies, whether a fixed fee is charged per payment, and whether the amount is billed monthly or prepaid. Keep revenue, fees, and payouts separate.

## A hypothetical fee comparison

Suppose a creator has $2,000 in monthly gross sales. For illustration only, assume option A charges a 10% platform fee, while option B has $100 in combined fixed platform and app costs. Ignore payment processing and other charges temporarily to isolate this one difference:

- Option A's modeled platform cost: $200.
- Option B's modeled fixed cost: $100.
- Equal cost on these assumptions: $100 ÷ 10% = $1,000 in monthly sales.

This is not a recommendation to migrate at $1,000. Restore the excluded fees and account for the work of replacing access, community, support, and billing. If the membership experience is materially worse, a theoretical fee saving can be a poor trade.

## Test the member journey before moving anyone

Create a map from each current benefit to its destination: posts, video, downloads, community, merchandise, and archived content. Record who receives access, when it begins, and when it ends. Identify data or entitlements that cannot be transferred directly.

Do not assume you can move saved payment methods or continue charging members on another platform without the required supported process and consent. Check the platforms' current migration guidance before committing to a transition date.

A small pilot should answer practical questions: Can a person buy? Can they find the promised resource? What happens after cancellation? Who fixes failed access? Keep the original service available until the migration plan can honor existing customer commitments.
