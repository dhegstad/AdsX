Before installing a Shopify app, check three possibilities: a feature already included in your store, an app provided by Shopify, and a third-party app. These are different implementation choices, and each needs to be evaluated against the actual job.

The best starting question is specific: “Can we offer this discount?”, “Can customers choose these bundle components?”, or “Can buyers receive this file?” A search for the broadest app category can lead to buying features you do not need.

## Use a three-option worksheet

| Option | Verify | Include in the cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Built-in setting | Plan eligibility, exact behavior, limits | Setup and ongoing operating work |
| Shopify-provided app | Installation, supported workflow, compatibility | Any charges and configuration work |
| Third-party app | Required features, permissions, dependencies, support | Subscription, usage, external services, and exit work |

Free software can take time to maintain. A paid app can be economical if it removes valuable repetitive work. Compare the complete workflow rather than the price label alone.

## Example: discounts versus bundle presentation

Shopify’s [discount documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/discounts) covers common discount codes and automatic discounts. If that satisfies the promotion, you may not need another tool to calculate the discount.

A bundle is a different requirement from simply reducing a price. Shopify’s [bundle eligibility guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/bundles/eligibility-and-considerations) distinguishes fixed bundles from mix-and-match needs and notes that Shopify Bundles does not support mix-and-match bundles. A shopper choosing components may require another solution.

Write the customer action first. “Buy two items and receive a discount” and “build a personalized kit from several choices” can belong to the same marketing campaign while requiring different software.

## Example: selling a file versus delivering a file

A digital product listing is not the complete delivery system. Shopify documents a free file-delivery app in its [Digital Products guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/digital-service-product/digital-downloads). Evaluate whether it supports your actual product and access expectations before comparing more complex tools.

For a single downloadable file, the checklist may be short. For memberships, licenses, or ongoing course access, map those requirements separately. Our [digital-product app stack](/blog/shopify-digital-products-app-stack-first-store) shows how to scope that evaluation.

## Run the same acceptance test for each option

Create a brief scenario with inputs, expected outcome, and failure cases. For a promotion, include eligible products, excluded products, a repeat customer, and any combination with another discount. For a workflow integration, include a missing field and a retry.

Record the result as verified, failed, or not yet checked. A demo video and a support promise are useful evidence, but they should not be marked as your store’s completed test.

Compare a normal operating day and the situation that produces the most support work. The edge case may be the reason the app exists: unusual delivery rules, a complicated catalog, or a large volume of changes.

## Make the decision reversible

Keep a record of the settings, content, and data involved. Identify an owner, a review date, and the steps to disable the feature. If an app replaces another tool, plan the handoff so both do not send duplicate messages or perform competing updates.
