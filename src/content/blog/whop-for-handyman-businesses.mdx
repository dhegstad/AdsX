A two-hour handyman booking can sound simple until the customer produces a full weekend’s task list. A useful Whop offer starts with a bounded work session: a known duration, an agreed list of suitable tasks, and a clear explanation of materials and unfinished work.

## Sell a time block with a reviewed task list

Ask the customer to describe the jobs before accepting the booking. Identify tasks you can undertake and those requiring a differently qualified trade or separate estimate. Avoid a checkout headline that implies every household repair fits one package.

A paid block should specify whether shopping, travel, setup, and cleanup consume the purchased time. If materials are additional, explain how they are approved and documented. “Materials extra” is not enough when one person expects a few screws and the other expects a new fixture.

| Offer boundary | Example wording to adapt |
| --- | --- |
| Time | Two hours at the property for agreed tasks |
| Task priority | Work through the accepted list in the customer’s order |
| Materials | Customer-approved purchases recorded separately |
| Unfinished work | Discuss a new booking or quote before continuing |
| Exclusions | Work outside your skills, scope, or authorization |

## Make the price calculation visible

Suppose a session costs an illustrative $160, with $35 of separately approved materials. The service and materials total is $195 before any other disclosed charges. If the customer already paid the $160 session price, the additional request should explain that only the materials remain.

## Leave room for the unexpected

If the first task reveals a problem that changes the job, stop and agree what happens next. Do not consume the whole block on an unapproved variation while the customer still expects the remaining list to be completed.

Keep notes on completed tasks, unused time if relevant to your policy, approved materials, and unresolved work. A simple job sheet in your existing system may be sufficient. A Whop client space is optional; it should make communication easier, not force a local customer to learn a new workspace for a one-off visit.

## Test the offer against actual time

A two-hour onsite session plus thirty minutes of travel and thirty minutes of administration occupies three working hours. At $160, gross revenue is about $53.33 per occupied hour before tools, vehicle costs, fees, insurance, and other expenses. Track those hours to decide whether the package price makes sense.

A defined task block is a better first experiment than unlimited household maintenance. Once a few jobs are complete, revise the intake questions around the surprises that actually occurred.
