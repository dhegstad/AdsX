## Turn the appointment menu into decisions

A first-time customer may not know whether they need a fill or a new set. Explain what each option includes, then ask enough questions to reserve appropriate time. Separate removing another technician’s work from a standard appointment if it changes your process.

For an illustrative menu, a basic set could reserve ninety minutes while a detailed-art set reserves two hours. Those are planning examples, not industry benchmarks. Use your own observed appointment times and include cleaning and preparation between clients.

| Customer choice | Detail to establish | Scheduling consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New set | Desired length and finish | Base service duration |
| Fill | Existing work and condition | Confirm whether a fill is appropriate |
| Removal | Material and current set | Add removal time when needed |
| Nail art | Design complexity agreed in advance | Reserve additional working time |

## Separate the reservation payment from the final price

If a customer pays $20 toward an $80 appointment, the service balance is $60 before separately disclosed charges. A reservation payment should not appear to be an extra fee unless that is actually your stated policy. Explain cancellation, late arrival, and rescheduling terms before the customer pays; the checkout itself does not make every policy enforceable.

Whop’s [one-time checkout links](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/payment-processing/create-checkout-link) can collect an agreed payment and ask questions. Keep a record of what that payment covers. If the customer changes from a plain finish to a much longer design, agree the new scope and appointment length before taking more money.

## Check the booking experience from a client’s phone

The official [Calendar Bookings walkthrough](https://whop.com/blog/calendar-bookings-app/) explains availability and duration settings. Evaluate whether it handles your exact appointment workflow. Do not assume it includes a salon point-of-sale system, automated no-show charging, chair allocation, or inventory tracking.

Walk through a booking as a customer. Can they find the address? Do they know whether they have paid in full? Can they request a design change without buying a second appointment? ## Start with your simplest repeatable service

One well-defined offer is enough for a pilot. A small menu helps reveal where clients misunderstand the process. Track booked duration against actual duration and note which intake questions would have prevented overruns.

Keep client images and service notes in an appropriate private system. Get permission before using a client’s hands or designs in public marketing. A community feed should never become the default place for private appointment details.

Whop is a candidate when your payment process needs structure and your scheduling needs are manageable.
