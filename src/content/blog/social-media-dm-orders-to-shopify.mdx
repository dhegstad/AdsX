Move social-media orders to Shopify when manual messages are creating preventable payment, stock, or delivery errors. Keep conversations where customers already contact you, but give each confirmed purchase a consistent order record and payment route.

The transition does not require shutting down your social accounts. It requires deciding which orders belong on a product page and which need an individually prepared quote.

## Choose the ordering path

| Type of request | Practical route | Information to settle first |
|---|---|---|
| Standard item with fixed options | Link to the exact product | Variant, quantity, delivery terms |
| Several regular products | Help the customer assemble a cart | Correct products and quantities |
| Custom quote or negotiated order | Prepare a draft order and invoice | Scope, price, stock, delivery |
| Question without purchase intent | Answer in the existing conversation | Whether an order is needed at all |

Shopify supports [draft orders and invoices](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/create-orders) for orders you prepare on a customer's behalf. Follow its [draft-order creation instructions](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/managing-orders/create-orders/create-draft) for the current controls. Draft orders and ordinary storefront checkout can have different delivery capabilities, so test the route you actually intend to use.

## Clean up the product information first

Build a small intake sheet before creating the catalog. Each sellable option needs a stable identifier, a customer-facing name, price, available quantity, and fulfillment promise. For a handmade bag, color and size may be selectable variants; a private note about the customer should not become public product content.

Start with the items you sell repeatedly. Importing every old conversation creates more cleanup work than a concise launch catalog. The [first catalog CSV guide](/blog/shopify-product-csv-import-first-catalog) explains how to validate a small import before expanding.

## Use one stock record

Decide when an unpaid inquiry reserves stock and when that reservation expires. A message saying “I'll take it” is not the same event as a completed payment. Keep the customer's promise, the order record, and the physical stock consistent.

If the same one-off item is listed on several channels, nominate the stock record that wins when quantities disagree. During the transition, reconcile every sale manually until your process or integration has demonstrated that it updates reliably.

## Give customers a clear handoff

A useful message is specific: “The blue medium bag is available. This product link shows the price and delivery options. Your order is confirmed after checkout.” For a custom order: “I've prepared an invoice for the agreed item and delivery. Please review the details before paying.”

These are examples to adapt, not messages that have been sent. Avoid implying that an unpaid order is already on its way. Never request card numbers in a DM or ask a customer to prove payment with sensitive account information.

## Test three realistic orders

Test a standard product, a multi-item purchase, and a custom quote. For each, check the confirmation, stock movement, delivery choice, and information available to the person packing the order.

Include one awkward case: the buyer changes the delivery address before fulfillment, wants a different variant, or misses the payment deadline. Write down who makes the change and how the customer learns about it. Use our [checkout test checklist](/blog/shopify-test-order-before-launch-checklist) to cover the payment and notification handoffs.

## Review the economics before expanding

Count the time spent chasing payments and correcting errors today. Then compare the proposed platform and app costs with the operating improvement you expect. A checkout link does not by itself create new demand, and it should not require a large app stack to process a straightforward order.

Build the monthly budget in the [startup cost calculator](/tools/shopify-startup-cost-calculator). Keep the first launch small enough to observe the full order cycle before directing every social-media conversation into the new process.
