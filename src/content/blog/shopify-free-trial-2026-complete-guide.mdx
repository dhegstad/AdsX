Shopify's public pricing page reviewed September 9, 2026 advertises three days free followed by $1 per month for three months. Treat that as the offer displayed on the reviewed page, not a guarantee for every country, account, plan, or signup route. Confirm the terms shown to you before accepting a subscription.

A free trial and a discounted paid period are different stages. The promotion gives you time to evaluate a store; it does not remove normal operating costs or prove the business will sell successfully. [Current Shopify pricing and offer](https://www.shopify.com/pricing).

## Understand the three billing stages

| Stage | What to verify | What not to assume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free trial | Length, start time, and available functionality | That every app or external service is free |
| Promotional paid period | Eligible plan, amount, currency, duration | That promotional pricing lasts indefinitely |
| Normal subscription | Renewal amount and billing cycle | That an annual monthly equivalent is charged monthly |

[Shopify's trial help page](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/pricing-plans/free-trial) says the trial begins when you sign up. It also explains that trial lengths and promotional details can vary. Save the offer shown during your own signup and the subscription details before confirming.

## Prepare before starting the clock

Have one representative product ready with images you can use, a clear description, options, and an estimated selling price. Know the main country you will sell from and the markets you intend to serve.

Write down the requirements that could prevent launch: an appropriate payment provider, the delivery method, an essential app or integration, and the operator's access needs. Resolve basic product questions through the [demand-validation guide](/blog/how-to-validate-product-demand-before-shopify-store) before spending the trial on a vague store idea.

You do not need a finished brand system to evaluate commerce. You do need enough product information to test a realistic order.

## What to test during the trial

Start with the [store-fit scorecard](/blog/shopify-free-trial-evaluation-scorecard). Build a representative product and check its options, price, images, and availability. Configure the payment and delivery arrangements you expect to use, then review the relevant plan and provider requirements.

Create the essential customer information: what is sold, how delivery works, how to contact the business, and the policies applicable to the offer. Do not copy another store's policies without checking whether they describe your business.

Test the storefront on a phone and follow the [order-to-delivery checklist](/blog/shopify-test-order-before-launch-checklist). A beautiful homepage is not a substitute for a working payment and fulfillment path.

## Can you sell during the free trial?

Shopify's trial documentation explains the need to select a paid plan to activate checkout and set up a payment provider to accept orders. Check the store's actual state before telling customers it is open.

A preview, a simulated test order, and a live customer purchase are distinct milestones. Choose the supported test method while configuring the store, then confirm the intended selling state before launch.

## What can cost extra?

Apps can have separate trials, subscriptions, or usage charges. Domains, paid themes, shipping labels, external services, and transaction-related costs may also be separate from the store promotion. [Shopify's billing reference](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/your-account/manage-billing/billing-charges) distinguishes recurring, usage-based, and one-time charges.

Before installing an app, read its pricing and the approval screen. “Free to install” is not the same as an unlimited free plan. Use our [app pricing explainer](/blog/shopify-app-free-to-install-vs-free-plan) to avoid that confusion.

Keep a small cost record with the vendor, amount, billing date, trial end, and cancellation route. A Shopify promotion should not obscure a separate app renewal.

## Compare the normal plan cost

Choose a plan that covers the required workflow at its normal price. Use the [plan-selection and cost guide](/blog/shopify-pricing-2026-every-plan-real-cost) to separate subscription, processing, apps, and operating expenses.

For illustration, a store with a normal subscription assumption of $40 per month and three promotional months at $1 would have $363 of subscription payments across those twelve monthly periods: $3 plus nine times $40. This is a hypothetical budget example, not a current Shopify plan quote. Other charges and the exact billing dates still need to be included.

A discount can reduce early spending, but the business should remain understandable after the offer ends.

## How to stop if the store is not ready

The trial help page explains what happens if you do not select a plan and how cancellation differs after choosing one. Review the current instructions for your store's stage, and check separate services or app commitments as well.

Keep exports or records you need before closing anything. Record the reason the store did not pass the evaluation so a later attempt starts with a concrete requirement to resolve.

If the store is ready, follow the [signup and setup walkthrough](/blog/how-to-sign-up-shopify-step-by-step) and retain your completed scorecard. The useful outcome is a verified store and a clear recurring cost, not simply claiming a promotion before a timer expires.
