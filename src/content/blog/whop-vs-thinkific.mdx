Whop is worth evaluating when your course is part of a paid community or a broader offer that combines education and services. Thinkific deserves a close look when the course delivery system is the center of your business, particularly when you need a clearly defined plan for assignments, memberships, or selling seats to organizations.

Both cover more than those starting points. The right choice depends on the learning experience you promise, the way you sell access, and the administrative work you can support. A platform's feature count cannot make that decision for you.

## Start with the learning promise

Imagine two businesses. A freelance designer sells a short course plus an ongoing critique group. A training company sells a structured program to teams, with assignments and a completion requirement. Both sell education, but their difficult operational questions differ.

For the designer, ask which conversations belong with each purchase and what happens when community access ends. For the training company, ask who buys seats, who studies, who reviews work, and who needs progress information. Write those requirements before opening either pricing page.

Our [Whop course-and-community bundle guide](/blog/whop-course-community-bundle) helps define separate access promises. A one-time course purchase should not accidentally imply unlimited personal coaching.

## Where Whop fits

Whop's [educational-program documentation](https://docs.whop.com/supported-business-models/educational-programs) describes combining Courses, Content, Forums, and Chat apps. Its Courses app supports module, chapter, and lesson organization, video and text lessons, knowledge checks, quizzes, completion requirements, and certificates. The guide also describes free, one-time, and recurring access options.

That makes Whop a reasonable candidate for the designer's course-plus-critique business. The evaluation still needs to establish the exact product-to-content access rules, the student's first steps, and the workload of moderating the group. A community is an ongoing service obligation, even when the lessons are prerecorded.

If you choose to explore that model, our [Whop course setup guide](/blog/whop-create-online-course) covers the structure. Do not equate a documented quiz or certificate feature with a guarantee that it satisfies a particular employer's training requirements. Bring the actual assessment and reporting requirements to the pilot.

## Where Thinkific fits

Thinkific's [current pricing page](https://www.thinkific.com/pricing/) lists Basic at $54 per month, Start at $109, and Grow at $219 when billed monthly in USD. Basic includes unlimited courses, a community, and a custom domain. Start adds assignments, completion certificates, memberships, subscriptions, and payment plans. Grow adds group orders, B2B selling, and more advanced reporting and integration capabilities. Annual billing has a different commitment and displayed rate.

For the training company, this creates a useful shortlist: investigate Start for structured learning and Grow when organizational buying is required. Confirm the exact seat-purchase, learner-assignment, and reporting workflows before committing. A plan listing “B2B” is the start of that conversation, not the entire specification.

Thinkific also includes community features, so this is not a simple choice between a community platform and a course platform. Likewise, Whop documents learning features beyond video hosting. Compare the specific implementation of the tasks you need.

## Compare the same operating model

| Decision | Question to resolve on either platform |
| --- | --- |
| Course delivery | Can learners follow the required sequence and find their next task? |
| Assessment | Can you review the type of work your course promises to assess? |
| Community | Which buyers receive access, for how long, and with what moderation? |
| Organizational buying | Can a buyer purchase for other people and manage the intended access? |
| Administration | Can staff resolve access problems without creating duplicate purchases? |
| Leaving the platform | Which content, customer, progress, and billing records can be transferred? |

For each answer, retain the current documentation or a result from your own evaluation. If you cannot verify a capability, mark it unresolved. Do not call it unavailable merely because it was absent from the first marketing page you read.

## Model fixed and variable costs separately

Whop's [standard pricing](https://whop.com/network/pricing/) lists no monthly or setup fee and domestic card processing at 2.7% plus $0.30. That is not an all-in rate: international cards, currency conversion, enabled services, payouts, and other events can add charges. Your account agreement and configuration determine what applies.

An illustrative month with 40 domestic-card purchases at $100 produces $4,000 in gross sales. At the displayed base rate, processing would be $108 plus $12, or **$120 before additional charges**. This example excludes refunds, disputes, taxes, enabled service fees, affiliate commissions, and payout costs. Use our [Whop fee guide](/blog/whop-fees-ecommerce) to build a fuller worksheet.

For Thinkific, begin with the plan your required workflow actually needs, then add the applicable processing, payment-service, and other charges. Confirm those through the current pricing and payment terms for your country. Comparing Whop's processing bill with only Thinkific's subscription price would leave out different categories on each side.

Use the same sales volume, payment mix, refunds, and staff assumptions in both columns. Add the time needed to support learners and maintain integrations. The cheapest platform invoice can still accompany an expensive manual process.

## Run a representative pilot before migrating

Build one module with a real lesson, the hardest assessment you need, and a sample access offer. Use each platform's supported evaluation or test process. Have a reviewer follow the learner journey on a phone and a laptop; record problems rather than claiming a complete review after viewing the dashboard.

Check purchase-to-access, lesson navigation, the next learning task, support access, and what happens when paid access ends. For a team-training business, add the organizational buyer and learner as separate roles.

If you already have students, inventory their access promises before moving anything. Confirm export and import support, recurring billing migration, progress records, content rights, and who will communicate the change. Do not cancel the old platform until the replacement and transition have been verified.
