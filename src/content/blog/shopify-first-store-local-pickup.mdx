A pickup-first Shopify store can give local customers a consistent way to order without creating a parcel-shipping operation on day one. The important work is defining where stock lives, when an order is ready, and how the customer knows it is time to collect.

Treat pickup as a fulfillment service with its own costs and capacity. A free pickup option at checkout does not make preparation, storage, customer support, or missed collections free for the business.

## Define the pickup promise

Before configuring the store, complete this operational brief. Use one real location and a manageable opening catalog for the first test.

| Decision | Example to adapt | Failure it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| Collection location | Clearly described counter or collection point | Customers going to the wrong entrance |
| Preparation time | A promise your team can consistently meet | Customers arriving before packing is complete |
| Collection hours | Actual staffed hours | Orders waiting at a closed location |
| Identification | Order number and your chosen collection check | Handing an order to the wrong person |
| Missed pickup | A stated contact and follow-up process | Unclaimed stock with no owner |

Avoid collecting extra personal information simply because a form can ask for it. Record the details necessary to locate and fulfill the order, and keep access limited to the team doing that work.

## Configure the location and pickup option

Shopify's [pickup-in-store instructions](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/setup/delivery-methods/pickup-in-store) require an appropriate fulfillment location and describe the pickup settings, preparation time, and customer instructions. Check your store's current checkout and plan capabilities before depending on a particular mixed-delivery feature.

Your product stock must correspond to the location preparing the order. If items are stored elsewhere, define whether a transfer is part of the promise and allow for the time it takes. Inventory at another location is not automatically ready to hand to a customer.

Use Shopify's [inventory documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/inventory) as the platform reference. For the opening catalog, the [product import checklist](/blog/shopify-product-csv-import-first-catalog) can help avoid variant and quantity errors.

## Work out the cost per collection

In a hypothetical $30 order, suppose product cost is $12, packaging is $1, preparation labor is $3, and payment processing is an assumed $1.20. Contribution before fixed expenses is $12.80. Ten such orders contribute $128; they do not necessarily cover the month's rent, software, or staffing.

Enter those inputs in the [startup cost calculator](/tools/shopify-startup-cost-calculator), using zero parcel postage when none applies. Put preparation and packaging in the relevant per-order cost. Keep fixed staffing in overhead if that better matches how you pay for it; do not count the same labor twice.

## Test the customer journey from a phone

Test an in-stock product, an unavailable product, and a basket whose items have different availability. Confirm that checkout displays the intended collection location and promise. Check the order confirmation and the separate ready-for-pickup message.

Then rehearse the handover. Can a staff member find the order, locate the packed item, and mark it collected? What happens if the customer sends someone else, arrives late, or requests a cancellation before collection?

These are operational test cases, not reasons to buy a scheduling app immediately. If you need booking slots or collection capacity limits, write that requirement down and verify an appropriate solution during the [trial evaluation](/blog/shopify-free-trial-evaluation-scorecard).

## Launch with a controlled service area

Make the location and hours obvious on the product pages and contact information. If pickup is the only delivery method, test an address outside the intended area so you understand what the customer sees. Resolve unavailable checkout paths before promoting the store.

Review the first week's orders for preparation time, missed collections, and stock discrepancies. Expand the catalog or add shipping after the pickup process is dependable, rather than trying to launch every delivery method at once.
