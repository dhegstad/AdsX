The blue bowl shows $18 beside a crossed-out $24 on its product page. In the collection grid the bowl looks full price. Before opening the theme editor, check what the cream bowl costs. The grid has to describe a product with both colors.

## Check the intended promotion

Write a row for every variant showing its current price, compare-at price, and whether it belongs in the promotion. Preserve an empty value as empty. Shopify distinguishes an empty compare-at field from zero. If only blue is discounted, a crossed-out $24 on every color would mislead someone buying cream. Keep the real prices and decide whether the card needs language such as “Selected colors on sale.”

If both colors should be $18, check the approved offer and correct only the inconsistent record. The selling price goes in Price and the higher original amount goes in Compare-at price. Shopify explains that conflicting compare-at values across variants can suppress the collection sale display even when the selected variant looks correct. [Shopify’s sale-price troubleshooting](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/details/product-pricing/sale-pricing)

## Follow a shopper from card to cart

Open the collection, click the card, select each color and add one to the cart. Record the visible badge, current price, crossed-out price and actual line price. Repeat on a phone, including quick add if the collection offers it. If the intended offer is a checkout code, record the code and its eligibility separately. A checkout discount operates at a different step from a catalog markdown. [Shopify’s discount FAQ](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/discounts/discounts-faq)

When variant data is consistent and the expected price survives checkout, compare the same collection in a duplicate theme preview with its standard card settings. If only the custom card fails, ask for an estimate to repair that component. A badge app is worth evaluating when the team also needs scheduled labels or product-specific messaging. It cannot decide which prices the merchant meant to promote.

For a hypothetical $15 monthly badge subscription, the annual fee is $180. A two-hour card change at $60 per hour plus one hour of retesting at the same rate also costs $180 in year one. The cheaper path depends on how often the theme changes and who maintains the promotion rules. Confirm those inputs before treating a new badge as a savings opportunity.
