A Big Cartel shop can move to Shopify without abandoning its identity. The practical challenge is preserving the details that make an artist's catalog work: editions, sizes, sold-out work, digital files, release timing, and the links already shared with customers.

Start with a reason to move. If your current shop meets your needs, staying is a valid choice. The [Big Cartel versus Shopify comparison](/blog/shopify-vs-big-cartel) helps distinguish a real operational requirement from a general desire for more features.

## Make a catalog inventory you can verify by eye

For each item, record its title, description, price, options, available quantity, images, and public URL. Add the details a generic product spreadsheet can miss: edition size, material, dimensions, artist credit, production lead time, and whether the item is a physical piece or a download.

For a small catalog, manual recreation may be more economical than buying a migration service. For a larger catalog, compare the time required with a tool's documented data coverage. We have not verified a universal one-click Big Cartel product-export route, so this guide does not depend on one.

Big Cartel does document [downloading order data as CSV](https://www.bigcartel.com/resources/help/article/download-order-data). An order export is useful for recordkeeping; it should not be mistaken for a complete product catalog or a ready-to-import Shopify order file.

## Map the artist-specific details

| Shop item | Detail to preserve | Practical check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Numbered print | Edition size and remaining quantity | The product page and stock record agree |
| Apparel | Each size and color combination | The buyer receives the selected variant |
| Made-to-order piece | Production time and customization | The order carries the instructions |
| Digital file | Asset, usage terms, delivery | A purchaser gets the intended version |
| Sold-out work | Portfolio value and old links | Visitors reach a useful archive or replacement |

Photograph or retain reference screenshots of unusual layouts before rebuilding them. Use those as a content checklist, not as a requirement to reproduce every pixel in a different theme system.

## Build five products before the entire shop

Create a representative pilot in Shopify, then inspect it on a phone. Check image order, cropping, option labels, stock behavior, and the text customers see before paying. A limited edition with the wrong quantity is a more serious error than a slightly different typeface.

If you use a spreadsheet, follow [Shopify's product import instructions](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/import-export/import-products). Test a small file and keep new products unpublished until their presentation and order behavior have been reviewed.

Do not label a download as a shipped physical item. Shopify's [Digital Products app documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/digital-service-product/digital-downloads) explains file and supported-link delivery. Review usage rights and test access with the exact file you plan to sell.

## Budget for the functions you already use

[Big Cartel's current plans](https://www.bigcartel.com/product/pricing) include physical and digital selling, with additional functions on higher tiers. A migration article should not assume that Big Cartel lacks digital products or abandoned-cart features.

List the tools actually used by your shop: print-on-demand connection, mailing list, discount rules, product drops, and shipping labels. Find the Shopify equivalent for each and write down any additional cost. If you rarely need a feature, it may not justify installing another paid app.

For a fictional maker spending two hours every month on a workaround, value that time explicitly before claiming a migration saves money. At an assumed $25 per hour, the time costs $50 monthly. Compare that with the additional software and setup cost, and replace both assumptions with your own numbers.

## Keep the audience's existing links useful

You can control redirects on a domain you own once it points at the new store. You should not assume you can create Shopify redirects for a `bigcartel.com` subdomain that remains hosted by Big Cartel.

Record links in social profiles, old campaigns, packaging QR codes, and artist biographies. Update the destinations you control. For your own domain, prepare the [Shopify redirect map](/blog/shopify-url-redirect-map-store-migration) before switching it.

Keep a clear path for customers asking about earlier orders. Decide whether the old shop needs to remain accessible for support while new purchases move to Shopify.

## Launch around the catalog, not the next product drop

Avoid making your first migration test coincide with a limited release. Verify an ordinary purchase and fulfillment workflow first. Check one domestic and one relevant international shipping address if you sell to both markets.

At the final switch, reconcile quantities for products still selling on Big Cartel. Confirm that the two storefronts cannot accidentally sell the same final unit. If both remain active, use a documented stock allocation or a verified synchronization process.

Work through the [store launch test](/blog/shopify-test-order-before-launch-checklist), then announce the new destination through channels you already control. A migration changes the selling system; it does not automatically bring a new audience to the work.
