A new Whop member should quickly find what they bought, complete one useful action, and know where to get help. Design the first week around those outcomes rather than a long welcome message or a tour of every feature.

## Start with the purchased promise

The first action depends on the product. A course learner might complete a setup exercise. A coaching client might submit a brief. A paid-community member might introduce a current problem in the correct discussion space.

Whop’s [product documentation](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/products/create-product) connects products to app access. Its [paid-group guide](https://docs.whop.com/supported-business-models/paid-groups) describes common community components. Use only the parts needed for the offer; every extra destination creates another place a newcomer can get lost.

## Build a first-week path

For an illustrative merchandising community, the journey could be:

| Timing | Member task | Evidence that the step worked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediately after joining | Find the start-here area and confirm access | Member can open the purchased experience |
| Day one | Share one current merchandising question | A relevant question appears in the intended space |
| By day three | Use one example or checklist | Member identifies a change worth trying |
| By day seven | Return with a result or follow-up | Discussion connects to the member’s actual work |

These are suggested milestones, not automated Whop events or guaranteed reporting fields. Record them through an appropriate manual review or supported analytics setup. Do not claim the dashboard measures a behavior until you have verified it.

## Write a short orientation

Give the member three things: the next task, the place to do it, and the support route if access fails. Put the main action before background information.

For example: “Post one product page you want to improve in the review forum, with the audience and the question you want answered.” That is more useful than “Introduce yourself and explore.” Explain what feedback is included and when it is normally provided.

Keep billing help separate from community discussion when it involves account or payment details. Members should not have to post private information publicly to solve an access problem.

## Check the handoff to external communities

If the purchase unlocks Discord or Telegram, include the correct connection instructions and a clear way to verify the account being linked. A successful checkout can still leave the buyer outside the community.

Use our [Discord access guide](/blog/whop-discord-paid-access) or [Telegram setup guide](/blog/whop-telegram-paid-group) to check that handoff. Test with a normal member view, since owner permissions can hide access problems.

## Measure progress without confusing the metrics

Count purchases, successful access, first useful actions, and return participation separately. A high number of purchases with low successful access suggests a technical or instruction problem. Good access with little participation suggests the first task or offer may be unclear.

Review a small group of recent members and record where they stopped. Avoid inferring intent from one missing action; someone may be busy or may already have received the value they needed.

Whop’s [analytics documentation](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/manage-business/analytics) describes dashboard measures. Our [tracking and analytics guide](/blog/whop-tracking-links-analytics) explains how to keep those measures separate from custom onboarding observations.

## Improve one point of friction at a time

If members cannot locate the first lesson, fix navigation before adding more content. If they find it but do not understand the exercise, revise the instructions. Keep the rest of the journey stable long enough to see whether the change helps.
