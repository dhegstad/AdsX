Start with a free Shopify theme if it can present your products clearly and complete the buying journey you need. A paid theme becomes easier to justify when it solves a specific requirement that would otherwise need an app, custom development, or substantial staff work. A polished demo alone is not evidence that it will increase sales.

This is a purchase framework, not a hands-on ranking of individual themes. Use your own product data and store requirements to compare candidates before committing money.

## Write the requirements before browsing demos

List the tasks your customer must complete. A five-product ceramics catalog may need dimensions, care details, and variant photos. A large spare-parts catalog may need more demanding navigation and compatibility information. The requirements should come from the products, not from whichever demo is most attractive.

Shopify's [theme selection guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/choose-themes) explains how to find themes by features and review support. Check the available capability on the actual candidate theme rather than assuming that every paid theme includes it.

## Compare with a small scorecard

Score each requirement as 0 for missing, 1 for workable with extra setup, or 2 for meeting the requirement with your sample catalog.

| Requirement | Weight | Evidence to collect |
|---|---:|---|
| Product information is understandable on mobile | 3 | Your longest realistic product page |
| Customer can find the right item or variant | 3 | A task completed using actual catalog data |
| Required apps display correctly | 2 | Theme preview with the intended app blocks |
| Staff can edit recurring content | 2 | A nondeveloper completes a normal update |
| Support and update process fit your resources | 1 | Developer documentation and support scope |

Multiply each score by its weight. A mandatory requirement that scores zero should remain a blocker even if the total score is high. This is an editorial scoring method; it does not assign untested scores to any vendor.

## Use the paid-theme preview correctly

Shopify lets you add a paid Theme Store theme to preview and customize before buying. You must purchase it before publishing, and a theme on trial has editing limitations. The [adding and buying themes documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/themes/adding-themes) also describes licensing and final-sale terms.

Use the preview for product layout, navigation, content, and supported integrations. After purchase and before launch, complete the actual order test. Do not treat a draft-theme screenshot as proof that checkout, fulfillment, or every integration has passed.

A paid theme can reduce one kind of work and introduce another. Record custom code, app dependencies, and who will maintain those changes when the theme updates.

## Compare total cost, not only the theme price

Suppose a paid theme costs a hypothetical $320 and replaces a $15 monthly app you would otherwise need. Ignoring all other differences, simple payback is $320 ÷ $15 = 21.3 months, so savings exceed the purchase price during month 22.

If setup also costs $180, the $500 combined investment takes 33.3 months to recover at the same monthly saving. If the free theme already provides the feature, there is no app saving to count. These figures are illustrations, not current theme or app prices.

Include setup, maintenance, app overlap, and the opportunity cost of delaying the launch. Do not count an assumed conversion lift as guaranteed revenue. Use observed customer behavior after launch to evaluate that hypothesis.

## Know when to defer the purchase

A free theme is a reasonable starting choice when the catalog is small, the product proposition is still untested, and customers can already find the information they need. Spend first on accurate product information, usable images, and a working fulfillment process.

A paid candidate deserves closer evaluation when its specific capabilities remove documented friction at a total cost you can support. Re-run the same scorecard when the catalog or customer requirements change.
