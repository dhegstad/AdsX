Expanding from Etsy to Shopify means building another selling channel, not automatically transferring Etsy's marketplace audience to your own website. You can keep an Etsy presence while developing a Shopify store, provided you manage inventory, customer obligations, and each platform's rules correctly.

[Shopify's Etsy migration guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/migrating-to-shopify/migrating-from-etsy) explicitly describes retaining Etsy as a channel. This guide focuses on the business decisions and verification work around that transfer. It does not use invented merchant success stories or revenue thresholds to tell you when to leave Etsy.

## Decide what the Shopify store will add

Write the reason for the new store in operational terms: a different catalog presentation, a direct audience you already reach, a required integration, or a way to support repeat customers through channels where they have chosen to hear from you.

Keep the value of Etsy discovery visible in the business case. A lower fee on an independently acquired order is not the same as replacing an order Etsy helped generate.

| Decision | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- |
| Keep Etsy and add Shopify | A stock plan and capacity to operate both |
| Move selected products | A reason for the channel assortment |
| Gradually emphasize Shopify | A verified independent acquisition path |
| Close Etsy eventually | A plan for outstanding orders, records, and lost discovery |

## Export the listings and inspect the scope

Follow the source-specific process in Shopify's guide. Etsy's listing CSV covers listings currently available for sale, so reconcile it with your intended destination catalog rather than assuming it includes every historical or inactive item.

Shopify also describes an early-access Store Migration app for eligible stores. If that route is unavailable, use the supported CSV or another verified method. Review the importer's limitations before relying on product options or market-specific prices.

Keep the original export and a working copy. Choose a pilot containing a simple item, a variation-heavy product, a made-to-order item, and a digital item if relevant. For an import-focused walkthrough, see [preparing an Etsy listing import](/blog/import-etsy-listings-to-shopify) and the [current Shopify CSV checklist](/blog/shopify-product-csv-import-first-catalog).

## Rebuild the details Etsy handled in the buying experience

A product listing needs clear dimensions, materials, options, production time, delivery expectations, and what is included. Your own store also needs enough business information for a new buyer to understand who is selling and how to get help.

For personalized goods, test how the buyer's instructions appear in the order. For downloads, verify the actual asset delivery using [Shopify's Digital Products documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/digital-service-product/digital-downloads).

Do not present a copied review as if it were collected through the new store. If you use a review-migration tool, verify permission, accurate attribution, and how it displays the source. A trust element should help the customer understand the evidence, not obscure it.

## Treat customer records and marketing separately

Retain records needed to serve earlier orders. An export containing an email address is not automatic permission to add that person to a promotional mailing list.

Review [Etsy's current seller policy](https://www.etsy.com/legal/sellers/) for rules on buyer information, communication, and off-platform transactions. Do not use an Etsy conversation to redirect an in-progress Etsy purchase elsewhere to avoid fees.

Build the independent audience through appropriate channels: your own content, social presence, and a clearly described opt-in on the new site. Track actual subscription consent and give people the communication they chose.

## Prevent overselling across channels

If both stores sell the same items, choose verified synchronization or assign stock deliberately. Document which system controls quantity, what happens when an order is cancelled, and how failures are noticed.

For a fictional maker with eight finished pieces, allocating all eight independently to Etsy and Shopify creates a risk of selling more pieces than exist. A stock allocation or tested connector is an operating requirement, not an optional enhancement.

Test the last-unit scenario, a cancelled order, and a product with variations. Review both channels after the test instead of checking only the Shopify inventory screen.

## Compare costs with acquisition included

Use your actual Etsy invoice and the [Shopify operating-cost worksheet](/blog/true-cost-running-shopify-store-first-year). Include subscriptions, payment costs, apps, marketing, and your time on both channels.

Do not apply one universal Etsy fee percentage to all orders. The relevant charges depend on the services and transaction circumstances. Similarly, do not assume the Shopify platform fee is the only cost of an independently acquired sale.

A sensible expansion test asks whether the new channel creates useful additional contribution and customer relationships, with operating work you can sustain.

## Launch a small, complete shop

Finish the buying and delivery journey for a coherent initial range. Verify product information, checkout, notifications, fulfillment, and support using the [launch checklist](/blog/shopify-test-order-before-launch-checklist).

A custom domain can support the independent brand, but Shopify cannot create redirects for Etsy's marketplace URLs. Update the links you control and keep a clear customer-service path for older orders.

## Review evidence before changing the channel mix

Record Shopify sessions, orders, contribution, repeat purchases, and support workload separately from Etsy results. A trial signup or a published storefront is not evidence that the new channel can replace existing sales.

Keep what works while you test the new route. Shift effort when the independent store's actual results justify it, rather than following a generic calendar that assumes every maker should abandon the marketplace.
