To sign up for Shopify, create or use a Shopify account, start a store, and work through the setup in your admin. Creating an account, selecting a paid plan, and opening a store for customer orders are separate milestones.

This guide follows Shopify's [current initial-setup instructions](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup). Screens and available offers can vary by location and account, so use the terms displayed in your own signup flow.

## Before opening the signup form

Have an email address you control, a proposed store name, an initial product idea, and a list of countries you intend to serve. Do not delay a small evaluation because you have not designed a complete brand identity. Equally, do not select an expensive app stack before understanding the basic store workflow.

Use the [startup cost calculator](/tools/shopify-startup-cost-calculator) to write down a budget ceiling. Keep any introductory subscription price separate from the ordinary operating cost after the promotion.

## 1. Start the account and store setup

Open the [Shopify signup page](https://www.shopify.com/free-trial). This is an affiliate link; AdsX may earn a commission from a qualifying referral at no extra cost to you. Use an existing account if appropriate, or create an account with a supported sign-in method.

Complete the displayed setup questions accurately. Where Shopify offers an optional question or a way to add billing later, choose the path that fits your evaluation. Do not use false business information to obtain a country-specific offer.

Keep the owner account secure and record which email controls it. Adding a contractor should not require handing over the owner's password.

## 2. Read the trial and billing terms

Before committing to a plan, record the trial end date, any promotional period, the ordinary renewal amount, and the billing cycle. Shopify's [free-trial guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/pricing-plans/free-trial) explains the distinction between evaluating the platform and choosing a plan to sell.

An app installed during a trial may have its own charges. Treat each approval as a separate purchase decision. Use the [trial evaluation scorecard](/blog/shopify-free-trial-evaluation-scorecard) to decide what you need to learn before paying for a longer commitment.

## 3. Add one representative product

Create a product that tests your real business requirements. If you sell clothing, include a size or color option. If you sell a download, verify the delivery method. If you sell a unique collectible, use the actual item and quantity.

Check the title, description, photos, price, stock, and physical or digital delivery settings. A single accurate product reveals more about the workflow than fifty incomplete placeholders. The [product import guide](/blog/shopify-product-csv-import-first-catalog) is useful when you are ready to expand the catalog.

## 4. Configure the essentials

| Area | Decision to make | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| Domain | Existing domain or a new registration | DNS records and renewal terms |
| Payments | Eligible provider for your business | Current fees and verification status |
| Delivery | Shipping, pickup, digital delivery, or a combination | A checkout showing the intended choices |
| Customer information | Contact route and accurate policies | Published pages visible from the store |
| Apps | Specific requirement that needs extra software | Reason, price, and owner for each app |

If you have an existing domain, follow the [domain and email checklist](/blog/connect-existing-domain-shopify-email-checklist) so a storefront change does not interrupt business email.

## 5. Test before sending customers

Work through a [test order](/blog/shopify-test-order-before-launch-checklist), including the customer email and fulfillment handoff. Check the store on a phone and confirm that a real customer can understand the product, delivery promise, and support contact.

Write down anything that remains unresolved before launch. A payment verification issue or missing delivery option matters more than polishing another homepage animation.

## What should happen next?

Review the [launch checklist](/resources/shopify-launch-checklist), finish the required account and plan steps, and open the store when the ordering workflow works. For the first week, compare actual costs and customer questions with your assumptions. Signup is the beginning of that learning process, not evidence that the business will automatically generate sales.
