> **Quick answer:** Signing up for Shopify takes about two minutes as of 2026: open [Shopify's free trial page](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624), skip or answer the short questionnaire, create your account with an email or passkey, and you land in your store's admin with the trial running — no credit card required. You pick a plan *after* the trial starts; Basic at $39/month (typically $1/month for your first 3 months) is the right call for almost every new store.

Shopify's signup flow is genuinely short, but it front-loads a few decisions that make new sellers freeze: a questionnaire that feels like a test, a store name field that feels permanent, and a plan picker with four price tags. None of those are real commitments — and knowing that in advance is the difference between a two-minute signup and an afternoon of second-guessing.

This is the full walkthrough: every screen, every decision, what's skippable, what's changeable, and what to do in your first hour once you're in.

## What do you need before signing up?

Almost nothing. Have ready:

- **An email address** you actually check (or a Google/Apple login, or a device that supports passkeys)
- **A rough idea of what you'll sell** — helpful for the questionnaire, but skippable
- **About 15 minutes** for signup plus the first-hour basics

What you do *not* need: a credit card, a registered business, a domain name, product photos, or a finished brand. Every one of those can come later. The [complete free trial guide](/blog/shopify-free-trial-2026-complete-guide) covers what the trial does and doesn't include, but the short version is that signup itself is free and cardless.

## Step 1: Start from the free trial page

Go to [Shopify's free trial page](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624?u=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.shopify.com%2Ffree-trial) and click **Start free trial**. You can start from almost any shopify.com page, but the trial page drops you straight into the guided flow instead of a marketing maze.

One thing worth knowing up front: the free trial itself is short. Shopify's model in 2026 isn't "test for a month free" — it's a brief free window to look around, followed by heavily discounted first months once you pick a plan. Treat the trial as the door, not the runway.

## Step 2: The onboarding questionnaire (yes, you can skip it)

Before creating your account, Shopify asks a handful of setup questions — the exact wording shifts over time, but as of 2026 they cover things like:

- **Where are you in your journey?** (just starting / already selling elsewhere / switching platforms)
- **Where would you like to sell?** (online store, social, marketplaces, in person)
- **What do you plan to sell?** (physical products, digital, services, dropshipping)

Here's what this questionnaire actually does: it customizes which setup suggestions and app recommendations you see first. That's it. It doesn't lock features, doesn't affect pricing, and isn't stored as some permanent profile you'll fight later.

Every screen has a **Skip** option (often "Skip all"). If you know your answers, give them — the tailored checklist is mildly useful. If you're just exploring, skip the whole thing without guilt. Nothing downstream changes.

## Step 3: Create your account

Next comes the actual account screen. As of 2026 you can sign up with:

- **Email** — enter it, and Shopify will have you verify and set up sign-in
- **Google or Apple** — one tap if you live in those ecosystems
- **Passkey** — Shopify supports passkey login, so you can skip passwords entirely on devices with Face ID, Touch ID, or a security key

Whichever you choose becomes your **Shopify ID** — the login that can eventually manage multiple stores, your billing, and (if you go that route later) partner and developer access. Use an address you'll still control in five years, not a throwaway or an old work email.

There's no credit card field on this screen. There won't be one until you deliberately choose a plan.

## Step 4: Name your store — and stop worrying about it

Somewhere in the flow, Shopify asks for a store name (or, if you skipped the questionnaire, it may generate a placeholder and let you rename). This is the field that stalls more signups than any other, so let's kill the anxiety with specifics:

- **Your store name is changeable anytime** in Settings → General. Takes ten seconds. Changes everywhere at once.
- **The `yourstore.myshopify.com` subdomain** created at signup is internal plumbing. Once you connect a real domain (step seven below), customers never see it.
- **Your actual brand** lives on your custom domain, your logo, and your theme — none of which exist yet.

Type something serviceable and move on. "Working title" is a legitimate strategy here; plenty of real stores launched under a different name than the one typed into this box.

## Step 5: You're in the admin — what just happened?

After the name step, Shopify provisions your store and drops you into the **admin** — the dashboard where everything happens: products, orders, settings, analytics. A setup guide checklist usually sits front and center.

Take stock of your position, because it's better than most people expect:

- Your free trial is **already running** — no plan chosen, no card entered
- You have a working (empty, password-protected) storefront
- Nothing you do in here can charge you money until you pick a plan and enter billing

Poke around freely. The worst-case outcome of this entire signup is that you walk away and the store goes dormant. If you'd rather explore with a plan, our guide to [what to set up during the trial](/blog/shopify-free-trial-what-to-setup-2026) sequences the trial window hour by hour.

## Step 6: Pick your plan — after the trial starts, not before

At some point — via the banner in your admin or **Settings → Plan** — you'll choose a plan. This is the first real money decision, and the most common mistake is overbuying. Here's the 2026 lineup:

| Plan | Monthly price (2026) | Online card rate (US) | Who it's actually for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | $5/mo | Higher than Basic | Link-in-bio and social selling only — no full online store |
| **Basic** | $39/mo | 2.9% + 30¢ | Almost every new store. Start here. |
| **Grow** | $105/mo | 2.7% + 30¢ | Established stores where the better card rate beats the price gap |
| **Advanced** | $399/mo | 2.5% + 30¢ | High-volume stores; the rate savings fund the plan |
| **Plus** | From ~$2,300/mo | Custom | Enterprise volume, multiple stores, checkout customization |

The default logic is simple: **pick Basic unless you can articulate exactly why you need something else.** The higher tiers earn their price through lower card rates and added features — which only matters once you have revenue for those rates to apply to. A new store paying $105/month to process $0 in sales has the math exactly backwards. The full breakdown of when each tier wins is in our [2026 plan-by-plan pricing guide](/blog/shopify-pricing-2026-every-plan-real-cost), and you can compare tiers side by side on [Shopify's pricing page](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624?u=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.shopify.com%2Fpricing).

Upgrading later takes two clicks and zero migration. Downgrading also works, but it's psychologically harder to claw back money you're already spending — another reason to start low.

## Step 7: Activate the $1/month promo

When you select Basic, Grow, or Advanced as a new store, Shopify's standard 2026 promotion applies: **$1/month for your first 3 months**. The activation is usually automatic in the plan-selection flow — the discounted price shows right on the plan cards — but verify the promo price appears on your confirmation screen before you confirm. This is also the moment you enter billing details; charges begin at the promo rate when your trial ends.

Three months at $1 is a genuinely meaningful runway: enough to launch, run real traffic, and see actual orders before full price starts. Our [$1-for-3-months deal guide](/blog/shopify-1-dollar-3-months-deal-2026) covers the fine print, including what happens at month four.

**Eligible for something better?** Founders who qualify for the 1MBB (1 Million Black Businesses) program can get a **120-day extended trial** instead of the standard short one — four full months to build before paying. If that might be you, read the [1MBB 120-day trial guide](/blog/shopify-120-day-free-trial-black-owned-businesses) *before* signing up through the standard flow, because the entry point differs.

## Your first hour: the four setups that actually matter

Account created, plan picked. The admin checklist will suggest a dozen things; these four are the ones that make the store real.

### 1. Domain

**Settings → Domains.** Either buy one through Shopify (fastest — DNS is handled for you) or connect one you already own from a registrar. Until this is done, your store lives on the myshopify.com subdomain, which reads as unfinished to customers and can't build brand equity.

### 2. Payments — and why Shopify Payments specifically

**Settings → Payments.** Activate **Shopify Payments** unless you have a specific reason not to. The case is concrete:

- **No extra transaction fee.** Using a third-party gateway on top of Shopify costs an additional 2% on Basic (1% on Grow, 0.6% on Advanced) *per order*. With Shopify Payments that surcharge is $0 — you pay only the card rate. (Weighing a gateway? Here's [Shopify Payments vs PayPal](/blog/shopify-payments-vs-stripe-vs-paypal) on the real fees.)
- **It unlocks Shop Pay**, Shopify's accelerated checkout. Buyers who've saved their details on any Shopify store check out in a couple of taps on yours, and Shopify publishes that Shop Pay converts better than guest checkout. You cannot enable Shop Pay without Shopify Payments.

Activation asks for identity and banking details (a legal name, address, and a bank account for payouts) — standard "know your customer" requirements, not a business-registration hurdle for sole proprietors in most regions.

### 3. One product

Not your whole catalog — one real product, with a photo, a price, honest inventory, and a description a human would read. One live product turns every later decision (theme, shipping, checkout test) from abstract to concrete. You can bulk-import the rest later.

### 4. Theme

**Online Store → Themes.** Pick a free theme and stop there. Free Shopify themes in 2026 are genuinely production-quality, and buying a $300+ premium theme before your first sale is one of the classic [beginner mistakes](/blog/shopify-beginner-mistakes-avoid) — you're styling a store that hasn't proven what it needs yet. Swap themes anytime without losing products or settings.

## What happens after signup?

The signup and first hour put you at "technically able to take an order." The gap between that and "launched" is a checklist, not a mystery: shipping rates, taxes, legal pages, a test order, and unpasswording the storefront. Work through the [first 30 days checklist](/blog/first-30-days-shopify-store-checklist) in order and you'll cover everything without spiraling into app-store browsing — which, for the record, is where new merchants most reliably lose their first week.

A note on when *not* to sign up: if you're selling a single digital offer through webinars and email funnels rather than a browsable catalog, or you only need a link-in-bio checkout, a full Shopify store may be more platform than you need right now (that's what the $5 Starter plan and funnel tools exist for). Shopify's strength is being the store you *grow into* — which is exactly why starting on Basic, cheap, is the low-regret move for everyone actually building a store.

## Bottom line

The entire Shopify signup, honestly summarized: two minutes to an account, no credit card, a skippable questionnaire, a store name you can change whenever, and a plan decision you make *after* you've seen the product — with Basic at $1/month for three months as the obvious default. The platform's onboarding is one of the least risky in commerce; the only real cost of signing up today is an hour of setup.

[Start your Shopify free trial](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624), skip the questionnaire if you're in a hurry, and spend your first hour on the four setups above — domain, Shopify Payments, one product, one free theme. Everything else can wait until there's a store worth optimizing.
