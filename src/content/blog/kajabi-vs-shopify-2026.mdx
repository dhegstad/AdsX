> **Quick answer:** Kajabi is built to sell knowledge — courses, coaching, memberships — behind a login. Shopify is built to sell products through a real storefront. If you sell physical goods, or knowledge *and* goods, [start with Shopify](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624) (free trial, then promotional pricing for your first months) and add courses via an app. If your entire business is a course catalog and community, Kajabi earns its higher price.

Searches for "Kajabi vs Shopify" keep climbing in 2026, and the two platforms confuse people because both promise to be the only tool you need. They are not competing for the same job. They are built around opposite answers to one question: **what are you actually selling?**

Kajabi assumes you sell **knowledge** — a course, a coaching program, a membership — delivered behind a login and sold through landing pages and email sequences. Shopify assumes you sell **products** — physical or digital — through a storefront customers browse, search, and return to.

Get that distinction right and the choice makes itself. Here's how to tell which one you are.

## What Kajabi actually is

Kajabi is a knowledge-commerce platform: course builder, membership and community hosting, video streaming, landing pages, email marketing, and checkout under one subscription. For a course creator or coach, that consolidation is the entire pitch — replace an LMS, an email tool, a funnel builder, and a community app with one login.

At what it's built for, Kajabi is genuinely strong. Courses support drip scheduling, quizzes, assessments, and completion tracking; memberships and communities live natively alongside the content; and the checkout handles offers, order bumps, and payment plans the way a launch-driven creator business needs.

What Kajabi is **not** is a store. There is no browsable product catalog, no collections, no inventory engine, no shipping-rate calculator, and no POS for selling in person. It also doesn't plug into the sales channels where physical products increasingly get discovered. Selling a few t-shirts or a book alongside your course means bolting physical fulfillment onto a tool that was never designed for it.

## What Shopify actually is

Shopify is the default way to build an online store in 2026 — millions of merchants, from single-product side hustles to billion-dollar brands. You get a real storefront with [genuinely usable free themes](/blog/best-free-shopify-themes-2026), a product catalog with variants and inventory, a checkout with Shop Pay's accelerated payments, shipping and tax tools, POS for in-person sales, and multi-channel selling that pushes your catalog to TikTok Shop, Instagram, Amazon, and Google from one admin.

Courses aren't native — and that's the honest gap. To sell a course on Shopify you install an app like Courses by Thinkific or Tevello, which adds lessons, drip content, and student access on top of your existing checkout. It works well and keeps one cart for courses *and* merch, but it's a component you add rather than a feature that ships in the box.

The trade-off is the mirror image of Kajabi's. Kajabi bundles knowledge-selling and makes physical products awkward; Shopify bundles product-selling and makes courses an app install. Which trade-off you want depends entirely on where your revenue comes from.

## Pricing: the comparison most reviews get wrong

Sticker prices make Kajabi look far more expensive — [$39/month Shopify Basic](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624) versus $179/month for Kajabi Basic ($143 billed annually). But the honest comparison includes what each price buys and how it scales:

- **Kajabi's price includes** course hosting, memberships, email marketing, funnels, and video. Rebuilding that on Shopify via apps might add $30–120/month depending on your course and email choices.
- **Shopify's price includes** an actual storefront, catalog, shipping, POS, and multi-channel selling — none of which Kajabi can add at any price.
- **Kajabi's tiers cap products, contacts, and admin users**, and the entry Kickstarter plan ($89/month) was pulled from public pricing in January 2026 — so the realistic floor is now Basic at $179. Crossing contact or product limits forces an upgrade toward $249 (Growth) or $499 (Pro).
- **Shopify's plan price is flat.** You [upgrade plans](/blog/shopify-pricing-2026-every-plan-real-cost) for lower card rates and advanced features, not because your email list or product count grew — and the [true first-year cost](/blog/true-cost-running-shopify-store-first-year) depends on the app stack you actually assemble.
- **Shopify's promotional onboarding** — free trial, then heavily discounted first months (see the [current deal](/blog/shopify-1-dollar-3-months-deal-2026)) — lets you validate an idea for almost nothing. Kajabi has no equivalent runway.

For a new business watching cash, that last point is usually decisive — you can stand up a real Shopify store for less than one month of Kajabi.

## The real decision: what are you selling?

**Choose Kajabi if all of these are true:**

1. Your product is knowledge — courses, coaching, memberships, or a paid community
2. You have no physical products, or never will
3. You want course delivery, email, and funnels bundled into one subscription
4. Your sales come from launches, webinars, and email — not browsing

**Choose Shopify if any of these are true:**

1. You sell physical products, print-on-demand, or merch — even alongside courses
2. You want a brand customers browse, search, and reorder from
3. You want to sell across TikTok, Instagram, Amazon, Google, or in person
4. You want the option to add courses (via an app) without giving up a real store

The pattern we see with creator businesses: a course does well, the audience asks for merch or a physical product, and suddenly the knowledge-commerce tool becomes the constraint. You can [sell digital products on Shopify](/blog/shopify-selling-digital-products) and add a course app, but you cannot make Kajabi ship a pallet of hoodies. Starting on the platform that can hold *both* sides of a creator business is cheaper than migrating later — and migrating *to* Shopify is well-trodden, whether from [Etsy](/blog/etsy-to-shopify-migration-guide) or another platform.

If you're weighing other creator platforms, the same logic runs through the cluster: [Kartra vs Shopify](/blog/kartra-vs-shopify-2026), [Shopify vs Teachable](/blog/shopify-vs-teachable-comparison), [Shopify vs Stan Store](/blog/shopify-vs-stan-store-comparison), and [Shopify vs Patreon](/blog/shopify-vs-patreon-comparison). Audience-and-funnel tools win for a single knowledge offer; Shopify wins the moment products and a brand enter the picture.

## Bottom line

Kajabi is excellent at the specific thing it does: selling courses, coaching, and memberships with the marketing stack bundled in. Shopify is the platform you build a product business on — far lower entry cost, unlimited catalog, real checkout, POS, multi-channel reach, and an app ecosystem that adds courses when you need them.

If you sell only knowledge and never physical goods, Kajabi's higher price buys real convenience. For everyone else — and especially anyone whose creator business is growing toward merch, bundles, or a catalog — [Shopify's free trial and promotional first months](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624) let you test a real store for less than a single month of Kajabi.
