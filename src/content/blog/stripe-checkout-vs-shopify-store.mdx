> **Quick answer:** Stripe and Shopify aren't competitors — Stripe is payments infrastructure, Shopify is a commerce platform (whose built-in Shopify Payments actually runs on Stripe under the hood). For 1–3 products, SaaS, or digital goods, Stripe Payment Links plus a landing page genuinely works. The moment you need a catalog, cart, shipping rates, tax automation, or abandoned-checkout recovery, [start a Shopify trial](https://shopify.pxf.io/c/6318547/3797171/13624) — that's platform work Stripe never claimed to do.

Most "Stripe vs Shopify" comparisons are broken from the first sentence, because they treat these as two versions of the same product. They're not even in the same layer of the stack. Stripe moves money. Shopify runs stores — and, in one of the internet's better ironies, Shopify's own built-in processor is powered by Stripe's infrastructure.

So the real question — the one indie hackers, founders, and side-hustlers are actually asking — is this: **"Can I skip the store? Can I just put a Stripe Payment Link behind a button on a landing page and call it a business?"**

Honest answer: yes, sometimes. And the "sometimes" has sharp, predictable edges. This comparison maps exactly where they are.

|---|---|---|

| **Monthly cost** | $0 platform fee | From [$5 Starter](/blog/shopify-starter-plan-five-dollars-review) / $39 Basic |
| **Per-transaction (US online, 2026)** | 2.9% + 30¢ standard | 2.9% + 30¢ on Basic via Shopify Payments (lower on higher plans) |
| **Storefront** | None — bring your own page | Full themed store (Basic+) |
| **Product catalog** | Individual links per offer | Unlimited products, variants, collections |
| **Cart** | No — single-offer checkout | Yes, multi-item |
| **Shipping rates** | Flat amounts only | Live rates, zones, discounted labels |

| **Abandoned checkout recovery** | No cart to recover | Built in |
| **Accelerated checkout** | Stripe Link | [Shop Pay](/blog/what-is-shop-pay-merchants-buyers) across millions of Shopify buyers |
| **App ecosystem** | Developer APIs and integrations | 8,000+ merchant-facing apps |
| **SEO surface** | Your one landing page | Product, collection, and content pages that rank |

## What Stripe actually is

Stripe is the payments layer a huge share of the internet is built on. Its core product is an API developers use to charge cards, manage subscriptions, and route money. On top of that, Stripe ships no-code tools — and those are what this comparison is really about:

- **Payment Links**: create a product and price in the Stripe dashboard, get a hosted checkout URL. Share it anywhere — a landing page button, an Instagram bio, an invoice email. No code, no monthly fee, standard processing rates.
- **Stripe Checkout**: the same polished, hosted checkout page, embeddable or redirect-based, for when you have a developer and a website.
- **Billing, invoicing, tax**: add-ons for subscriptions, invoices, and sales tax calculation — capable, but priced as extra per-transaction fees on top of processing.

What Stripe deliberately is **not**: a storefront, a catalog, a cart, an inventory system, a shipping engine, or a marketing stack. Stripe assumes something else — your app, your site, your platform — handles all of that and hands it the final step: the payment.

## What Shopify actually is

Shopify is that "something else," plus the payment. It's the default way to build an online store in 2026: storefront themes, a product catalog with variants and inventory, a multi-item cart, checkout with [Shop Pay accelerated payments](/blog/what-is-shop-pay-merchants-buyers), shipping zones and discounted labels, sales tax calculated at checkout, analytics, and an app store with 8,000+ apps covering everything from email flows to bundles.

Payments are built in via **Shopify Payments** — which, as of 2026, runs on Stripe's infrastructure under the hood. That detail matters for two reasons. First, it means the "Stripe vs Shopify" fee fight is mostly an illusion: on Shopify Basic, Shopify Payments charges the same 2.9% + 30¢ for U.S. online card transactions as Stripe's standard rate. Second, it means you *can* plug your own Stripe account into Shopify as a third-party gateway — but Shopify adds an extra fee per transaction (2% on Basic) when you bypass Shopify Payments, so almost nobody should. We've broken down that whole decision in [Shopify Payments vs Stripe vs PayPal](/blog/shopify-payments-vs-stripe-vs-paypal).

## Can you really sell with just Stripe Payment Links?

Yes — and for a specific kind of business, it's the right call, not a compromise.

The Stripe-only stack looks like this: a landing page (anything from a free one-pager to a ~$20/month site builder), a Payment Link behind the buy button, and Stripe's dashboard as your "back office." Total platform cost: roughly zero to $20/month, plus processing.

That stack genuinely works when:

1. **You sell 1–3 fixed offers.** One SaaS plan and an annual option. A digital template. A consulting package. Each gets its own Payment Link, and managing three links is trivial.
2. **The product is digital or a service.** No shipping rates to calculate, no inventory to track, fulfillment is an email or an account provision.
3. **Pricing is simple.** Fixed amounts or straightforward subscriptions — Stripe Billing handles recurring charges well.
4. **Traffic comes from an audience, not browsing.** A newsletter, a launch tweet, a podcast plug. Visitors arrive pre-sold on one specific thing; nobody needs to compare products.

This is the same logic we applied in [Kartra vs Shopify](/blog/kartra-vs-shopify-2026): when the sale happens on a single page with a single offer, you don't need a store — you need a checkout. Stripe's is excellent, and it's the cheapest legitimate way to take money on the internet.

## Where does the Stripe-only stack break?

Every one of these is a real wall we see founders hit — usually in this order.

### Catalog and variants

Payment Links are one-offer objects. Ten products in three sizes and four colors isn't ten links — it's a combinatorial mess of links nobody can browse, search, or filter. Shopify treats variants, collections, and inventory as first-class objects because that's what a catalog *is*.

### No cart

Stripe's hosted checkout sells one thing at a time. There's no "add to cart, keep browsing, buy three items in one order." For any business where order size matters — and average order value is the lever that makes paid ads profitable — the missing cart is a silent revenue cap.

### Shipping rates

Payment Links can tack on a flat shipping amount. That's it. No weight-based rates, no zones, no live carrier quotes, no discounted label purchasing. Sell physical goods on Stripe alone and you're either overcharging customers or eating the difference on every order.

### Taxes

Stripe Tax exists, but it's an add-on that charges an additional fee per transaction, and filing is on you or yet another tool. Shopify calculates sales tax at checkout on every plan as part of the platform — one less thing bolted on.

### Abandoned checkout recovery

There's no cart in the Stripe-only stack, so there's nothing to recover — a shopper who bounces from a Payment Link is simply gone. Shopify tracks abandoned checkouts and emails those shoppers automatically. If you're paying for traffic, recovery isn't a nice-to-have; it's margin.

### Shop Pay

Shopify checkout offers [Shop Pay](/blog/what-is-shop-pay-merchants-buyers), the accelerated wallet shared across millions of Shopify buyers — returning customers check out with saved details in a couple of taps, and Shopify publishes that it converts meaningfully better than guest checkout. Stripe has its own accelerated option (Link), but it doesn't come with Shopify's buyer network or the Shop app surface around it.

### Apps and everything after the sale

Reviews, bundles, subscriptions-with-swaps, back-in-stock alerts, returns portals, post-purchase surveys — on Shopify these are an app install. On the Stripe-only stack, each is a custom integration project or simply doesn't happen.

### SEO surface

A landing page is one indexable URL. A Shopify store generates product pages, collection pages, and a blog — dozens or hundreds of pages that can rank and pull free traffic for years. If organic search is anywhere in your growth plan, a one-page site starts that race standing still.

None of these are Stripe failing. They're the definition of the layer Stripe doesn't operate in.

Three things worth pulling out of that table:

- **Processing is basically a wash.** Stripe standard and Shopify Payments on Basic both charge 2.9% + 30¢ for U.S. online cards — and Shopify's rate *drops* on higher plans (2.7% on Grow, 2.5% on Advanced), which Stripe's standard pricing doesn't do without negotiated volume deals. The full rate card is in our [transaction fees explainer](/blog/shopify-transaction-credit-card-fees-explained).
- **The real delta is $5–39/month.** That's the entire price gap between "Stripe plus a page" and "an actual commerce platform." One recovered abandoned checkout or one slightly larger cart per month typically covers it.
- **Starter is the underrated bridge.** At [$5/month](/blog/shopify-starter-plan-five-dollars-review), Shopify Starter gets you real product pages, Shopify checkout, and Shop Pay without a full storefront — a genuine middle step between a Payment Link and a $39 store, with the same upgrade path when you outgrow it.

## When should you genuinely stay Stripe-only?

Not everyone should migrate, and pretending otherwise would be bad advice. Stay on Stripe Payment Links if:

1. **You're a SaaS.** Your product *is* the website; Stripe Billing plus Checkout is the industry-standard stack, and a Shopify store adds nothing.
2. **You sell a service or one or two digital products** and your list or audience does the selling. A funnel page and a link beat a store here — the same verdict we reached for funnel-first businesses in [Kartra vs Shopify](/blog/kartra-vs-shopify-2026).
3. **You're validating an idea this weekend** and genuinely don't know if anyone will pay. A Payment Link is the fastest possible test — though note Shopify's free trial plus promotional first-months pricing makes the "real store" test nearly as cheap in 2026.
4. **You need payments inside a custom app** — marketplaces, platforms, anything where developers are building the buying experience. That's Stripe's home turf, full stop.

Everyone else — physical products, growing digital catalogs, anyone planning to browse-and-reorder rather than launch-and-blast — hits the walls above, usually within months, and rebuilds on a platform anyway. Starting there is cheaper than moving.

## Bottom line

Stripe and Shopify aren't rivals; they're layers. Stripe is world-class payments infrastructure, and for 1–3 products, SaaS, or services sold to an existing audience, Payment Links plus a landing page is a perfectly honest business stack — cheap, fast, and unbloated. Shopify is the commerce platform built a layer above (running its own payments on Stripe, no less), and it earns its $5–39/month the moment you need a catalog, a cart, shipping rates, taxes, recovery emails, or Shop Pay.
