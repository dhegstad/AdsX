Choose a product review app by the customer evidence you need to collect and display, the operating work involved, and the cost at your expected order volume. A high star rating in the App Store is one input; it does not tell you whether the app fits your catalog and theme.

This guide provides a selection method. It is not a hands-on ranking or a claim that one vendor converts better than another.

## Define the minimum useful workflow

Write one sentence describing the job: “Collect product reviews from actual buyers, show them on the correct product, and let our team respond to problems.” Then add only the requirements that affect your store.

| Requirement | Question for each candidate | Evidence from your test |
|---|---|---|
| Collection | What triggers a request and how are duplicates handled? | One supported sample journey |
| Product mapping | How do variants and grouped products behave? | Review displayed on the intended item |
| Media | Which photo or video features are included? | Mobile upload and display check |
| Moderation | How are spam, private data, and disputes handled? | Documented review workflow |
| Import | Can existing reviews be imported with valid provenance? | Sample import and mapping check |
| Export | Can you retrieve text, media references, and metadata? | Usable sample export |
| Theme fit | Does the widget work with your current theme? | Mobile and desktop inspection |
| Cost | What changes with orders, requests, or features? | Quote at normal and peak volume |

Do not import reviews from unrelated products or invent customer experiences. Preserve the origin of imported reviews and follow the platform's and app's rules for displaying them.

## Build a short candidate list

The official listings for [Judge.me](https://apps.shopify.com/judgeme) and [Loox](https://apps.shopify.com/loox) provide current feature and pricing information for two candidates in this category. Use them as starting points, then compare additional apps if a required workflow is missing.

Write down which plan includes the specific feature you need. “Photo reviews supported” is incomplete if the plan you budgeted does not include the required workflow. Confirm any separately billed services and the effect of higher order volume.

Our [app cost audit](/blog/shopify-app-costs-audit-guide) provides a register for subscriptions and variable charges. Keep that register alongside the scorecard.

## Test one realistic product journey

Use a supported test process or preview; do not create public fake reviews to test the widget. Inspect the request experience, submission form, display, moderation controls, and export options. Test a product with variants if your store depends on them.

Check text size, contrast, image loading, and whether the widget interrupts product selection on mobile. A desktop screenshot alone does not show how the page behaves on a small screen or a slower connection.

Record the number of steps needed to handle a disputed review or correct a product mapping. Operational friction can matter more than an extra visual layout option.

## Score requirements without hiding a failure

Mark each essential requirement pass, fail, or unverified. Only score preferences after the essential requirements pass. A weighted total should not allow a missing export or broken product mapping to disappear behind attractive design scores.

For costs, compare the same period and usage. Hypothetically, a $15 fixed plan costs less than a $5 base plus $0.20 per request once monthly requests exceed 50, assuming there are no other differences. Actual app plans may use different units, allowances, or tiers; use their documented formula.

## Plan for a future change

Before committing, document how you would export reviews, remove the widget, cancel billing, and confirm the storefront still works. Our [app review evaluation guide](/blog/how-to-evaluate-shopify-app-reviews) helps assess other merchants' experiences, while the [uninstall cleanup checklist](/blog/uninstall-shopify-apps-cleanup-guide) covers removal.

Choose the app that passes the workflow tests at an acceptable total cost. Revisit the decision when requirements change, rather than replacing a working app whenever a new feature appears elsewhere.
