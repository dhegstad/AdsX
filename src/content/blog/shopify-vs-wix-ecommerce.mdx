Wix deserves consideration when a business combines a website, services, bookings, and a product catalog. Shopify deserves consideration when commerce operations are central and its supported store workflows fit the business. The right choice depends on what the website must do every week, not simply how its homepage looks.

This is a requirements-based comparison using official information reviewed September 9, 2026. We have not conducted a controlled performance or conversion test of the two platforms.

## Define the business before choosing the builder

Write down the five most common customer actions. For a clothing brand, they may all involve buying and tracking products. For a photographer, they may include viewing a portfolio, requesting a session, reserving a date, buying a print, and downloading an image.

Those are different websites. Comparing only the cheapest ecommerce subscription ignores the work that surrounds checkout.

| Business pattern | Evaluate first | Critical test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Services with some merchandise | Wix's relevant business plan | Booking and product purchase work together |
| Product-first store | Shopify's suitable store plan | Catalog, fulfillment, and returns are manageable |
| Content site adding a small shop | Both, including the existing site | Content workflow remains practical |
| Complex operational integration | Whichever has verified support | Your actual external system receives the order |

“Evaluate first” is a research order, not a promise that the other platform cannot do the job.

## Compare plans that actually accept payments

[Wix's current plan page](https://www.wix.com/plans) distinguishes Light from Core, Business, and Business Elite. The displayed ecommerce/payment capabilities belong to the relevant business tiers. Do not compare a basic website plan with a fully operational store plan.

Wix also states that prices and currency vary by location and that displayed subscription terms matter. Shopify's [pricing page](https://www.shopify.com/pricing) likewise needs to be read for the merchant's market and billing period.

Create a quote with the date, currency, billing frequency, included capabilities, and normal renewal price. Add scheduling, digital delivery, email, and any other software required by the actual business. A free domain for the first year should not be budgeted as a domain that never renews.

## Build two complete customer journeys

For a fictional ceramics studio, test these two paths:

1. A customer buys a ready-made bowl, selects the appropriate delivery option, and receives the correct order information.
2. A customer books a workshop, selects an available session, and receives the instructions needed to attend.

Write down every manual step used to connect these paths. If the business offers only products, replace the workshop with its hardest product configuration or return scenario.

The platform that looks easiest in a design demonstration may require more operating work after orders arrive. Conversely, a sophisticated catalog system may be unnecessary for a service business with five merchandise items.

## How much content flexibility do you need?

Shopify includes a blog with its online store, as documented for the [Basic plan](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/pricing-plans/plans-features/basic-shopify-plan). That does not prove its editorial workflow matches an existing Wix site page for page.

List the content types you actually publish: portfolios, service pages, articles, galleries, FAQs, or member resources. Build one realistic example of each important type and ask the intended editor to update it.

Avoid choosing on a generic “better SEO” claim. Both the quality of the content and the implementation of useful pages, links, and metadata matter. A platform change is not a substitute for answering customer questions.

## Include the operator's time in the cost

Assume a fictional service business saves 30 minutes each week by keeping bookings and website updates in a familiar workflow. At an illustrative $30 hourly value, that is about $60 across four weeks.

That figure is not a Wix or Shopify performance result. It demonstrates why a small subscription difference may matter less than the process the operator repeats. Replace the assumptions with observed work before making the business case.

## What changes if you are already on Wix?

A migration should account for store data, bookings, member access, custom logic, forms, and valuable pages. [Shopify's Wix migration documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/migrating-to-shopify/migrating-from-wix) provides the product-transfer route, but additional functions still need review.

Use our [Wix migration guide](/blog/migrate-wix-to-shopify-complete-guide) to prepare the scope. Keep the current business functioning until a replacement passes its tests.

For a new store, use the [Shopify evaluation scorecard](/blog/shopify-free-trial-evaluation-scorecard) and compare it with the same practical tests on Wix. Choose the platform whose actual plan and tools cover the business at an acceptable cost, with an operator who can maintain it.
