The best Shopify bundle app depends on the offer and the way your team fulfills it. Start by comparing **Shopify Bundles** for fixed kits, **Bundler** for discount-driven offers, and **Simple Bundles & Kits** for configurable kits and component-level fulfillment needs.

This shortlist is based on official listings and Shopify documentation reviewed October 4, 2026. The recommendations below are editorial judgments about fit; we have not run a hands-on performance comparison of these apps.

## Compare the offer, price, and operational fit

Listed monthly prices are in USD, checked on the review date. Confirm the price and features shown for your store before approving a charge; annual billing options are not used in this table.

| App and primary source | Use case to evaluate | Listed price | Check before choosing |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Shopify Bundles](https://apps.shopify.com/shopify-bundles) | Fixed kits and multipacks | Free | Whether its bundle model supports your selling workflow |
| [Bundler](https://apps.shopify.com/bundler-product-bundles) | Quantity discounts, Buy X Get Y, and mix-and-match offers | Free plan; Premium $9.99/month; Executive $19.99/month | Required offer type and reporting tier |
| [Simple Bundles & Kits](https://apps.shopify.com/simple-bundles) | Configurable kits and component fulfillment | Free: 3 bundles and 50 monthly orders; paid tiers $14, $39, or $149/month by Shopify plan | Order allowance, plan eligibility, and fulfillment integration |

### Shopify Bundles: evaluate it first for a fixed kit

Shopify's own app creates fixed bundles and multipacks, lets customers select component variants, and updates inventory in real time. A paid third-party app is not automatically necessary for component inventory tracking.

Use it as the first candidate for a predetermined kit, such as a notebook plus a pen. Its scope is narrower than a customer-built box: Shopify's [bundle requirements](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/bundles/eligibility-and-considerations) exclude mix-and-match from this app and identify purchase-option incompatibilities, including subscriptions. Do not assume a subscription box will work because it is also called a bundle.

### Bundler: compare the discount rules

Bundler's free tier lists volume discounts, quantity breaks, and Buy X Get Y. Premium adds mix-and-match, while Executive lists bundle analytics. That makes it a candidate when the offer rule is the main requirement.

Before selecting a tier, write the exact offer in plain language: “Choose any three eligible items, receive this discount, and combine it with these other promotions.” Then verify the resulting cart. A widget that looks right does not establish that discount combinations or inventory behave as intended.

### Simple Bundles & Kits: inspect the fulfillment output

Simple Bundles advertises bundle breakdown into component SKUs, inventory synchronization, and build-your-own-bundle functionality. Its paid monthly tiers are tied to Shopify plans: $14 for Basic, $39 for Grow and Advanced, and $149 for Plus. The free plan has the limits shown above.

Treat the fulfillment workflow as an acceptance requirement. Give the warehouse a sample order and ask whether its existing process can pick, pack, partially refund, and restock the kit correctly. Confirm the app's order-count definition and behavior at the free-plan limit before relying on that tier.

The [free-to-install versus free-plan guide](/blog/shopify-app-free-to-install-vs-free-plan) explains why a free listing label is not a complete cost comparison.

## Check contribution before promising an AOV increase

AOV measures revenue per order, not the money available after variable costs. Use this simplified planning formula:

**Contribution per order = revenue after discounts − product cost − payment fees − variable fulfillment and shipping cost − allocated app cost.**

These hypothetical USD figures illustrate a decision; they are not results from any app:

| Item | Current order | Bundle offer |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue after discounts | $50 | $65 |
| Product cost | $20 | $32 |
| Payment fees | $2 | $2.50 |
| Variable fulfillment and shipping | $7 | $9 |
| Allocated app cost | $0 | $0.50 |
| Contribution before acquisition and overhead | **$21** | **$21** |

The bundle raises order revenue by 30% but leaves contribution unchanged. If a deeper discount reduces bundle revenue to $60 with the same assumed costs, contribution falls to $16. Acquisition cost, returns, and overhead still need consideration.

Allocate a fixed app subscription across the orders expected to use it, then check the result at lower volume. For example, a hypothetical $20 monthly subscription costs $0.50 per bundle order at 40 orders, but $2 at 10. Keep those assumptions in your [app cost register](/blog/shopify-app-costs-audit-guide).

## Test inventory and checkout with one representative bundle

Shopify's inventory calculation uses the limiting component and the quantity required per bundle. For an illustrative kit containing two notebooks and one pen, 11 notebooks and eight pens permit five complete kits. Shopify excludes components from this calculation when inventory is untracked or continuing sales while out of stock is enabled. Verify those settings before treating the displayed availability as protection against overselling.

Run the following acceptance checks with the chosen app and the actual integrations you intend to use:

| Scenario | Evidence to save |
|---|---|
| A component sells individually | Remaining bundle availability matches the component quantities |
| The limiting component runs out | Storefront availability follows the intended stock policy |
| A shopper changes or removes an item | Cart contents, quantities, and discount amounts remain correct |
| Another discount is applied | The combination matches the offer terms and margin calculation |
| An order reaches fulfillment | The warehouse receives the required component identifiers and quantities |
| A component is returned | Refund amounts, eligibility, and restocking follow the intended process |
| A shopper uses mobile or another market | Selection controls, currency display, and checkout remain usable |

Test subscriptions, preorders, POS, or other channels separately when they are part of the requirement. Different apps can implement bundles differently; an advertised integration is a reason to investigate, not proof that every configuration works.

## Choose the smallest setup that passes your checks

Begin with one offer and record its product choices, price, variable costs, app tier, and acceptance results. Compare overall conversion, order contribution, returns, and operational errors as well as bundle sales. An app dashboard's attributed bundle revenue does not show what those customers would otherwise have purchased.

Use the [app-review evaluation checklist](/blog/how-to-evaluate-shopify-app-reviews) to investigate recurring compatibility concerns. If replacing an existing tool, follow the [app removal and billing checklist](/blog/uninstall-shopify-apps-cleanup-guide) so overlapping subscriptions and leftover code do not become part of the experiment.
