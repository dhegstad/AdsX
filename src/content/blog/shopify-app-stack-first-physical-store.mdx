A first physical-product store needs a working order journey before it needs a large app stack. Begin with products, payment, delivery, and customer support. Add an app when the required workflow is missing or the manual work has a measurable cost.

Shopify’s [new-store checklist](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/new-to-shopify-checklists/general-checklist) provides the official setup sequence. This guide focuses on the app decisions around it, rather than repeating every admin setting.

## Build the stack around four jobs

| Job | What to establish first | App evaluation trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present the catalog | Products, variants, photos, and a usable theme | A required merchandising workflow is missing |
| Take payment | Supported providers, markets, and a tested checkout | A specific payment or selling requirement needs another solution |
| Deliver orders | Inventory ownership, shipping process, and fulfillment responsibility | Your carrier, warehouse, or order volume needs integration |
| Support customers | Contact route, policies, and order information | Support volume or complexity exceeds the current process |

A subscription product, made-to-order item, or warehouse integration can change the minimum stack. Define those requirements before adopting a generic list of “must-have” apps.

## Work through one product and one order

Pick a representative product with the variants and delivery requirements you expect to sell. Document how stock is recorded, how an order reaches the person fulfilling it, and how the customer receives updates.

An app should fit that process. A beautiful storefront widget cannot repair an unclear fulfillment handoff. Equally, manual fulfillment can be reasonable at a small volume if someone owns it and checks it consistently.

Use a worksheet with five columns: task, current method, observed problem, proposed app, and acceptance test. “Install a review app” is a proposed solution. “Collect and display real customer feedback after orders are delivered” is the job you need to evaluate.

## Check Shopify’s own features before adding a subscription

For standard promotions, Shopify supports discount codes and automatic discounts. Start with its [discount documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/discounts), then investigate apps when your actual requirement exceeds that workflow.

Some Shopify-provided functionality still uses an installed app. For example, product bundles require a bundles app, as explained in the [bundles guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/bundles). “Provided by Shopify” and “already configured in my store” are different facts.

Our [built-in features versus apps guide](/blog/shopify-built-in-features-vs-apps) gives a practical decision process. Do not assume either a native feature or a paid app is always the better choice.

## Put a price on the work you want to remove

Suppose a fictional app costs $30 per month and could save three hours of repetitive work. At an assumed $20 per hour, the potential time value is $60. That makes it worth evaluating, but only if the app actually removes the work without adding another equally expensive task.

Keep revenue claims separate from time savings. An app listing’s promised sales benefit is a hypothesis for your store, not a line you can put into a forecast as guaranteed income.

Record expected monthly usage, busy-month usage, trial end dates, and external services. See [free-to-install versus free plans](/blog/shopify-app-free-to-install-vs-free-plan) for an example of costs changing with volume.

## Add optional categories after the core journey works

Reviews, loyalty, advanced email automation, subscriptions, bundles, and advertising integrations can all be relevant. Choose the next category from a business need: missing repeat purchases, a proven recurring product, support demand, or a campaign you are ready to measure.

Before launch, complete an order-flow review and resolve the failures. After launch, use the [first 30 days checklist](/blog/first-30-days-shopify-store-checklist) to prioritize improvements.
