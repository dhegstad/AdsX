## What is Shop Pay?

Shop Pay is Shopify's accelerated checkout. The first time you use it, it saves your email, phone number, card, and shipping address. Every time after that — on *any* Shopify store with Shop Pay enabled, not just the one where you signed up — it recognizes you, texts you a one-time code, and completes the purchase with a single tap instead of a dozen form fields.

That cross-store memory is the whole point. A normal "save my card" feature only helps on the site where you saved it. Shop Pay works across the millions of stores running on Shopify, so a buyer who opted in once at a sneaker brand can check out in seconds at a coffee roaster they've never visited. For buyers it kills form-filling; for merchants it means a large share of first-time visitors arrive with checkout details already on file.

Shop Pay launched in 2017 (originally as "Shopify Pay") and in 2026 it's one of the most widely seen checkout buttons on the internet — which is exactly why so many people are googling it.

## Shop Pay vs the Shop app vs Shopify Payments: what's the difference?

People conflate three Shopify products with similar names, and most confusion about Shop Pay is really confusion about which layer does what. Here's the disambiguation:

| | Shop Pay | Shop app | Shopify Payments |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Accelerated checkout button | Mobile shopping and package-tracking app | Payment processor (behind the scenes) |
| **Who interacts with it** | Buyers, at checkout | Buyers, on their phone | Merchants, in their Shopify admin |
| **What it does** | Autofills saved payment + shipping details | Tracks orders, resurfaces brands, enables reorders | Processes card transactions and payouts |
| **Costs anything?** | Free for buyers; no merchant surcharge | Free | Standard card rates (from 2.9% + 30¢ on Basic) |
| **How they connect** | Requires the store to run Shopify Payments | Uses Shop Pay as its built-in checkout | Powers Shop Pay; replaces third-party gateways |

In one sentence: **Shopify Payments** processes the money, **Shop Pay** speeds up the checkout, and the **Shop app** is a buyer-facing app that has Shop Pay built in. ## How does Shop Pay work for buyers?

Shop Pay works in two phases: a one-time opt-in, then one-tap checkouts everywhere else. Here's the actual flow.

**First purchase:** at checkout on a Shopify store, you enter your details normally and tick the box to save your information with Shop Pay (or you're prompted with your phone number). That creates your Shop Pay profile — email, phone, card, addresses.

**Every purchase after:** when you hit a checkout on any Shop Pay-enabled store, it recognizes your email or phone, texts you a six-digit code, and presents an order summary with everything pre-filled. Confirm, done. On repeat visits from the same device it can be a single tap.

A few practical details buyers ask about:

- **Where it works:** Shopify stores with Shopify Payments, plus the Shop app. Not Amazon, not non-Shopify sites (though as of 2026 Shopify has started extending it to select large retailers off-platform).
- **Managing your data:** you can view saved cards and addresses, update them, or delete your Shop Pay account entirely through the Shop app or shop.app settings.
- **Cost:** nothing. Same prices, same refund rights — it's an autofill layer, not a wallet holding your money.

## Is Shop Pay safe?

Shop Pay is safe in the ways that matter: it's operated by Shopify (a public company processing payments for millions of stores), card details are stored encrypted on PCI-compliant infrastructure rather than by the individual store, and every checkout is verified with a one-time SMS code — so someone who knows your email still can't spend your saved card without your phone.

In one respect it's *safer* than typing your card into a small store's checkout form: with Shop Pay, the merchant never handles your raw card number. Shopify does, once, and vaults it.

The honest caveat: **Shop Pay secures the checkout, not the merchant.** The button tells you the payment rails are Shopify's; it doesn't tell you the store ships quality products on time. A drop-shipper with three-week delivery can offer Shop Pay just like a great brand can. Check the store the way you normally would — reviews, policies, contact info — and if something goes wrong, your card issuer's dispute process still applies as usual.

Two hygiene notes: Shop Pay's texts only ever contain a code, never a link asking you to "verify your account" — treat those as phishing. And since your phone is the second factor, standard SIM-security practice (a carrier PIN) is worth having anyway.

## What is Shop Pay Installments?

Shop Pay Installments is the buy-now-pay-later option inside Shop Pay, available at checkout on eligible US Shopify stores. Instead of paying in full, buyers can split a purchase into four interest-free payments, or — on larger orders — choose longer monthly plans, which may carry interest depending on the terms offered at checkout.

How it works in practice, kept at the level that stays true as terms shift:

- **Eligibility** is decided at checkout with a soft inquiry through Shopify's lending partner (Affirm, in the US) — it doesn't affect the buyer's credit score to check.
- **The split-in-four option is interest-free**; monthly plans on bigger purchases can carry interest, and the exact terms are shown before the buyer commits.
- **Merchants get paid in full up front**, minus fees; the lending partner takes on the repayment risk.
- **Merchant cost:** installment orders carry a higher processing fee than standard card payments — that's the trade for offering financing without building any of it.

For merchants selling at higher price points ($100+), installments are often the strongest single argument for Shop Pay: they convert the "I want it but not $300-this-week" shopper without you touching credit risk. If you're selling $20 accessories, it matters much less.

## Shop Pay vs PayPal vs Apple Pay: which accelerated checkout wins?

Shop Pay, PayPal, and Apple Pay all solve the same problem — skip the forms — but they differ in where they work, what the buyer needs, and what they cost the merchant. Most Shopify stores offer two or all three, and that's usually the right call.

| | Shop Pay | PayPal | Apple Pay |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Works on** | Shopify stores (+ Shop app) | Almost every platform | Supported sites, on Apple devices/Safari |
| **Buyer needs** | Phone number + one-time opt-in | A PayPal account and login | Apple device with card in Wallet |
| **Checkout feel** | SMS code, one tap | Redirect to PayPal login, approve, return | Face ID / Touch ID, instant |
| **Installments** | Shop Pay Installments (eligible US stores) | PayPal Pay Later options | Varies by card issuer/lender |
| **Merchant cost on Shopify** | Included in Shopify Payments rates, $0 extra | PayPal's own processing rates | No surcharge; runs through your existing rates |
| **Buyer protection route** | Card issuer dispute / store policy | PayPal's dispute process + card issuer | Card issuer dispute / store policy |

The practical read: **Apple Pay** is the fastest for iPhone users but invisible to everyone else. **PayPal** has the broadest buyer trust and its own dispute system, which some shoppers specifically prefer. **Shop Pay** is the only one purpose-built for Shopify's checkout — and the only one whose saved-details network is made of people who already buy from Shopify stores.

For merchants, the fee mechanics matter more than the buttons: run everything through Shopify Payments and there's no extra platform fee; route payments through a third-party gateway instead and Shopify adds 0.6%–2% per transaction depending on your plan. ## Why does Shop Pay matter for merchants?

For merchants, Shop Pay attacks the single biggest leak in e-commerce: checkout abandonment, especially on mobile. Most abandoned carts die in the gap between "add to cart" and "payment complete" — every field a thumb has to fill on a phone screen is a chance to quit. Shop Pay collapses that gap to a code and a tap.

Three reasons it converts:

1. **Fewer fields, less friction.** A returning Shop Pay user skips essentially the entire form. On mobile, that's the difference between a 30-second checkout and a 3-minute one.
2. **The network effect works in your favor.** Because profiles are shared across all Shopify stores, buyers who have never heard of your brand can still arrive with saved details. You inherit checkout speed you didn't build.
3. **Installments raise the ceiling.** For eligible US stores, higher-ticket carts get a financing option at zero engineering cost.

Shopify publishes its own research on this and the claim is directional but consistent: Shop Pay checkouts convert meaningfully better than guest checkouts, with the biggest lift on mobile. Treat the exact percentages as marketing, but the mechanism is real — it's the same reason Amazon patented one-click checkout decades ago.

Shop Pay isn't the whole checkout story, of course. Express buttons sit on top of the fundamentals — clear shipping costs, few surprises, a checkout that agents and assistants can also navigate as [agentic checkout becomes a real channel in 2026](/blog/agentic-checkout-optimization-shopify). But of everything you can do to a Shopify checkout, flipping on Shop Pay has the best effort-to-impact ratio: the effort is a checkbox.

## How do you enable Shop Pay on your store?

Shop Pay comes with Shopify Payments — there's no separate application, approval queue, or add-on fee. If you're on Shopify and using Shopify Payments, you may already have it. To check or enable it:

1. In your Shopify admin, go to **Settings → Payments**.
2. Under Shopify Payments, click **Manage**.
3. In the wallets section, make sure **Shop Pay** is checked (it typically is by default). Enable Apple Pay and Google Pay while you're there — they cost nothing extra.
4. Save. The purple button appears on your product pages, cart, and checkout automatically.

The one real prerequisite is Shopify Payments itself, which requires being in a supported country and passing standard identity verification during setup. If you're currently on a third-party gateway, switching to Shopify Payments gets you Shop Pay *and* removes the 0.6%–2% third-party transaction fee — for most stores that's a double win, with the trade-offs covered in our [Shopify Payments vs Stripe vs PayPal comparison](/blog/shopify-payments-vs-stripe-vs-paypal).
