Shopify Payments, a separate Stripe account, and PayPal are not interchangeable switches available to every Shopify merchant. Start with the payment options actually offered for your business and country, then compare total fees and the customer experience.

The practical default is to evaluate Shopify Payments where eligible and consider additional payment methods your customers need. A universal claim that one setup always converts better or always costs less would need evidence from your own store.

## What is different about each option?

| Option | Role in a Shopify store | First thing to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Shopify Payments | Integrated payment service managed through Shopify | Business eligibility, supported country, account requirements |
| Separate Stripe integration | A third-party option only where available in Shopify | Whether it is offered for your location and configuration |
| PayPal | Wallet or checkout integration, depending on country | Which integration Shopify supports and who handles settlement |

Shopify's [provider documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/third-party-providers) distinguishes direct providers from providers that send customers to an external checkout. That difference belongs in your checkout test, alongside cost.

## PayPal setup depends on location

The [PayPal Express Checkout documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/paypal) currently says Express Checkout is unavailable to merchants in the United States and France; merchants there can offer PayPal Wallet through Shopify Payments. Other locations can have a different setup.

This matters when following an old tutorial. A missing button may reflect the country or integration rather than a broken account. Check the current help page and the options in Settings → Payments before making changes. Do not deactivate a working provider just to reproduce someone else's screenshots.

## Build a total-fee worksheet

Record these inputs from your live agreements. Keep payment types separate when they carry different rates.

| Input | Example assumption | Your actual quote |
|---|---|---|
| Payments processed | $5,000 across 100 payments | Fill in |
| Processing percentage | 3% | Fill in |
| Fixed charge per payment | $0.30 | Fill in |
| Additional platform percentage | 0%, or 2% where applicable | Fill in |
| Currency conversion and other fees | Excluded from this example | Fill in |

Under the example assumptions, processing costs $150 plus $30, or $180. A separately applicable 2% platform fee adds $100. The resulting $280 is not a quote for Stripe, PayPal, or Shopify Payments; it demonstrates why comparing only the processing percentage can miss a material expense.

Shopify documents exclusions from third-party transaction fees for PayPal and manual payments when Shopify Payments is activated. That does not mean PayPal itself is free, or that every third-party provider receives the same exclusion. Confirm how your particular integration is billed.

## Compare payment flow as well as price

Run a [checkout test](/blog/shopify-test-order-before-launch-checklist) for each enabled method. Check mobile layout, delivery choices, address handling, confirmation messages, and the order's payment status. A displayed wallet logo is not proof that a payment can settle successfully.

For each method, record who resolves a dispute, where the payout appears, and how a refund is initiated. If a separate dashboard is involved, assign an owner. Plan how customer support will identify an order without asking customers to send sensitive payment information.

A normal payout timetable does not guarantee that every future payout arrives on that schedule. Verification reviews, weekends, reserves, and account-specific issues can affect available cash. Avoid spending tomorrow's expected payout today without a buffer.

## When does a higher Shopify plan pay for itself?

Use the difference in subscription cost divided by the reduction in the applicable fee rate. In a hypothetical comparison, an extra $60 per month and a 0.2 percentage-point reduction imply $30,000 in eligible monthly payment volume before the rate saving covers the extra subscription.

Use eligible volume, not every dollar of reported sales. Different payment methods, refunds, fixed charges, and negotiated rates can change the result. The [startup cost calculator](/tools/shopify-startup-cost-calculator) helps model the store's broader margin, while the [hidden-cost guide](/blog/hidden-shopify-costs-how-to-avoid) covers expenses beyond payments.

## A safe selection sequence

Confirm business eligibility first. Obtain the current processing and platform fee schedules. Test each needed payment method. Reconcile a real permitted payment, refund, and payout when your store is ready. Finally, review the blend after a meaningful period of trading.

Keep a dated record of the configuration and rate terms. Choose a setup because it works for your customers and operations, then revisit it when payment mix or volume changes.
