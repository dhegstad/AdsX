Choose Shopify when you want a hosted commerce platform and its supported workflows fit the business. Choose WooCommerce when WordPress integration, code-level control, or an existing WordPress operating setup is central to what you sell. Neither is automatically the cheaper option once maintenance, extensions, and payment costs are included.

This comparison uses current official documentation reviewed September 9, 2026. The decision examples are editorial illustrations, not hands-on performance benchmarks or merchant results.

## The central difference is responsibility

[WooCommerce](https://woocommerce.com/pricing/) is a free, open-source ecommerce platform for WordPress. You choose hosting and the extensions needed by the store. A free core license does not pay for the infrastructure or the people who maintain it.

Shopify includes hosting within its store subscription. Its [Basic plan documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/intro-to-shopify/pricing-plans/plans-features/basic-shopify-plan) describes a customizable online store, product and order management, and a built-in blog. Shopify still requires someone to manage apps, catalog quality, theme changes, and the business itself.

| Decision | Shopify | WooCommerce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hosting responsibility | Platform-managed store hosting | Merchant chooses a hosting arrangement |
| Core software cost | Store subscription | Free core; budget for the surrounding stack |
| WordPress relationship | Separate platform; content can be rebuilt or integrated | Commerce runs within WordPress |
| Customization approach | Themes, apps, and supported platform interfaces | WordPress/WooCommerce code and extension ecosystem |
| Operational test | Do supported workflows meet your requirements? | Can your team maintain the selected stack reliably? |

The table describes operating models. It is not a claim that every WooCommerce store needs expensive development or that Shopify eliminates technical work.

## Compare a complete monthly budget

Write down hosting or platform cost, paid extensions/apps, payment processing, support, maintenance labor, and any external services. Use the [current Shopify quote](https://www.shopify.com/pricing) for your market and your actual WooCommerce supplier quotes.

Here is a fictional comparison to show how labor changes the result:

| Monthly expense | Hosted-store scenario | WordPress-store scenario |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Platform or hosting | $40 | $25 |
| Apps or extensions | $20 | $15 |
| Maintenance time at $30/hour | 1 hour: $30 | 3 hours: $90 |
| Total before processing and business costs | $90 | $130 |

These are assumptions, not vendor prices. If you already maintain WordPress and the incremental work is half an hour, the second scenario becomes $55. That reversal is why a universal “WooCommerce is free” or “Shopify always saves money” verdict is unhelpful.

Also compare processing contracts and any platform-level transaction fees separately. A lower monthly subscription can be outweighed by costs tied to the payment routes your customers actually use.

## When WooCommerce is a strong fit

Consider WooCommerce when the existing WordPress site already attracts the right audience, when your business depends on a particular WordPress workflow, or when you have a reliable maintainer who understands the stack.

For example, an established specialist publisher adding a small catalog may gain little from splitting content and commerce into separate systems. Test the actual purchase and support journey before assuming a new platform is an improvement.

Code access is valuable when it supports a real requirement. It is less useful when nobody has the time or skill to maintain the customization after launch.

## When Shopify is a strong fit

Consider Shopify when selling products is the site's main job, you want to consolidate daily commerce work, and required functionality can be demonstrated using the available plans and apps.

A merchant whose weekly workload includes repeated hosting or plugin problems should quantify those incidents. If a move removes the source of that work, the operating benefit may justify a subscription even when the current software license costs nothing.

Avoid assuming that every custom checkout, subscription, or membership arrangement can be recreated with a single app. A platform constraint matters most when it touches a must-have business rule.

## Run the same five tests on both options

1. Build the hardest representative product, including its real options.
2. Place a suitable test order through the payment and delivery workflow.
3. Process the kind of return or cancellation your customers need.
4. Have the intended operator update stock and product information.
5. Record the normal ongoing cost and the person responsible for failures.

Use identical scenarios so the comparison is fair. A polished demonstration on one platform and an unfinished experiment on the other proves little.

## Switching an existing store

Shopify's [WooCommerce migration documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/migrating-to-shopify/migrating-from-woocommerce) describes available transfer routes. Product data, historical records, theme work, plugins, and URLs still need separate review.

Our [WooCommerce migration checklist](/blog/how-to-migrate-from-woocommerce-to-shopify) turns that into a practical release plan. If starting fresh, use the [store-fit scorecard](/blog/shopify-free-trial-evaluation-scorecard). Choose the platform that passes your requirements at an acceptable operating cost; do not move a functioning store solely because another platform has a longer feature list.
