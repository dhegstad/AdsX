A Squarespace to Shopify migration should preserve the business's useful catalog, content, and customer workflows while changing the commerce platform. Exporting products does not move the entire website, and connecting the domain does not by itself transfer email, bookings, or historical records.

Start by defining the improvement you want: a required commerce workflow, an integration, or an operating problem you can measure. There is no universal revenue threshold or migration timetable that makes the move right for every Squarespace merchant.

## Inventory the site before exporting

List products, variants, images, pages, blog posts, forms, downloads, scheduling, memberships, and integrations. Add the current public URLs and the business owner for each important function.

| Source component | Destination decision | Verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product catalog | Import supported data and rebuild missing detail | Representative variants display and order correctly |
| Editorial content | Retain or improve useful pages | The new page answers the same customer need |
| Scheduling or services | Keep or replace the service workflow | A complete booking/request test |
| Memberships or gated material | Define supported access and transition | Existing entitlement is handled accurately |
| Forms | Rebuild fields and routing | A submission reaches the intended team |
| Domain and email | Connect the website while preserving required services | Web and mailbox checks both pass |

Give special attention to functions that create customer obligations. A gallery can be rebuilt visually; an active membership or future booking needs a continuity plan.

## Use a supported product-transfer route

[Shopify's Squarespace migration guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/migrating-to-shopify/migrating-from-squarespace) explains the platform-specific export and import process. Follow the guidance for the source data available in your account and review the transfer limits before assuming every product type or custom field is included.

Save the source export unchanged. Use a working copy and a small pilot containing the products most likely to reveal mapping problems: several variants, a sale item, a sold-out option, and a special fulfillment requirement.

The [product-import checklist](/blog/shopify-product-csv-import-first-catalog) helps distinguish products, variants, identifiers, and stock checks. Keep images and downloadable assets accessible through a dependable destination before closing the source store.

## Verify the catalog as both buyer and operator

Inspect the imported pilot in the Shopify storefront. Compare title, description, images, options, price, stock, and delivery information with the intended product.

Then review a representative order. Confirm the person fulfilling it can identify the item and selected options. If a product is made to order, check that lead times and customer instructions survive the new buying journey.

For a fictional catalog of 12 product designs with several size options, matching the 12 product titles is only one part of validation. The purchasable size combinations and their quantities need their own reconciliation.

## Rebuild content around useful destinations

Preserve pages that answer customer questions or support product discovery. Recreate the navigation and review how the new theme presents images, galleries, and longer articles on a phone.

Do not assume a Squarespace page layout or template can be imported as a working Shopify theme. Choose a destination design and move the content into it deliberately.

If a blog serves an existing audience, prioritize its important destinations and internal links. Keep original publication dates where appropriate, and use an updated date only when the content has actually changed.

## Prepare relevant redirects

Collect old URLs before the source site becomes inaccessible. Map product, category, and content destinations individually where their paths change. [Shopify's redirect documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/menus-and-links/url-redirect) describes the supported routing behavior and limitations.

Use the [redirect-map worksheet](/blog/shopify-url-redirect-map-store-migration) to record why each destination is relevant. Test unusual source paths and any market-specific routes rather than assuming a bulk upload handles every case.

Do not promise a fixed search-recovery date. A migration can change how search engines crawl and understand the site; preserving useful content and correct routing helps, but it does not establish a guaranteed ranking outcome.

## Connect the domain and preserve other services

[Shopify supports connecting a third-party domain](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/domains/add-a-domain/connecting-domains). A registration transfer is not required solely to change where the storefront is hosted.

Record which provider handles DNS and which handles email. Keep the required mail records and active mailbox service. Follow the [domain/email checklist](/blog/connect-existing-domain-shopify-email-checklist) before cancelling any old subscription or bundle.

If you intend to move registration later, treat it as a separate task with its own provider requirements. There is no need to combine every account change into the storefront launch.

## Launch when the requirements pass

Record the last catalog export time and reconcile stock and orders received after it. Complete the [test-order checklist](/blog/shopify-test-order-before-launch-checklist), including notifications and any external fulfillment process.

Confirm important pages, old links, contact routes, and the public domain after the switch. Assign someone to review the first real orders and support requests.

Keep Squarespace access while it is still needed for historical records, unresolved orders, bookings, or service obligations. The migration is finished when the business can operate reliably from the intended systems, not when the old subscription disappears from the budget.
